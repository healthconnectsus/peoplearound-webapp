# Thirty ideas — marketing, development, business development

*Written 2026-09-18, overnight. Ten each. Companion to
[IDEAS_2026-09.md](IDEAS_2026-09.md) (42 product ideas) — nothing here repeats
that list, [MARKETING.md](MARKETING.md), [BUSINESS.md](BUSINESS.md) or
[OUTREACH_TARGETS.md](OUTREACH_TARGETS.md); where an idea leans on one of
them it says so.*

Every idea has the same five lines: what it is, why it fits *this* product
now, the first concrete step, rough effort, and how you'd know it worked.
They are written to pass the house rules already on file — no points, no
leaderboards, no paid reach between neighbors, nothing that names a private
person publicly without their say-so ([INCENTIVES.md §4](INCENTIVES.md),
[BUSINESS.md §4](BUSINESS.md)).

## If you only pick three

1. **D1 — move the database off the smallest tier.** It is the reason pages
   feel slow, it costs about the price of a lunch per month, and nothing else
   on this list matters if the product feels broken when someone finally
   arrives. The evidence is below; it took most of the night to find.
2. **B1 — social prescribing with one clinic or agency on aging.** The
   product is, underneath, a loneliness intervention with an audit trail.
   Nobody else selling to that buyer has attested, local, in-person
   participation as their native data.
3. **M1 — "This weekend in {city}", public, with a no-account Thursday
   email.** The crawler already fills it (70 upcoming listings across Boulder
   and Kansas City today). It is the first thing on the site worth visiting
   for someone who has never heard of it.

## Marketing

### M1. "This weekend in {city}" — a public page and a Thursday email

**What.** `/city/[slug]/weekend`: the next seven days of imported local
listings, grouped by day, readable without an account, with one line at the
bottom: *"Going to one of these? See which neighbors are."* Plus a single
field — email, no password — for a Thursday-morning send of the same list.

**Why now.** The supply exists and is unused outside the login wall: 49
upcoming listings in Boulder and 21 in Kansas City, refreshed every 48 hours,
each carrying its source's own tags. `/city/[slug]` is already public and
already in the sitemap, but it shows counts, and counts give nobody a reason
to come back on Thursday. Every competing "things to do" page is an ad farm;
this one is a plain list from the parks department and the library.

**First step.** Add the listings block to the existing city page (the query
is `LocalCalendars` minus the membership filter, through an anon-safe view
like `public_cities`). Email capture second.

**Effort.** Page: half a day. Email list + send: two days (Resend and the
digest cron are already wired).

**Worked if.** Organic search clicks to `/city/*` go from ~0 to weekly
double digits within six weeks; ≥15% of email subscribers create an account
within a month.

### M2. Project story pages — the thing a founder is proud to post

**What.** When a project is marked complete, generate `/stories/[id]`: a
public, permanent page with the title, the before/after photos from the
build log, the timeline, and the credited team — each person shown only if
they tick "show my name publicly". An Open Graph card to match.

**Why now.** [MARKETING.md](MARKETING.md) already says every finished
project is a local news story, and [OUTREACH_TARGETS.md](OUTREACH_TARGETS.md)
says not to pitch media until one exists. Neither says what the reporter
*clicks on*. Today it would be a login page.

**First step.** One flagship project, written by hand as a static page, to
learn what belongs on it before templating.

**Effort.** Three days including per-person consent.

**Worked if.** Completed projects get shared by their own founders without
being asked; referral traffic from those links converts at >10%.

### M3. Pin campaigns to civic days — starting with 28 September

**What.** A standing calendar of days on which people are already disposed
to do something for a neighbor, each with one tiny, specific ask that the
product makes easy: **National Good Neighbor Day (28 Sep — ten days from
now)**, Make a Difference Day (fourth Saturday of October), MLK Day of
Service (third Monday of January), Earth Day (22 Apr), National Night Out
(first Tuesday of August).

**Why now.** Small help — asks that take minutes — is the feature with the
lowest barrier, and these days hand you the reason to try it. It is also a
reason to email existing members that isn't "we miss you".

**First step.** For the 28th: one email and one feed banner — *"It's Good
Neighbor Day. Ask for, or offer, one small thing."* — pointing at the small
help composer. Nothing new to build.

**Effort.** Two hours per campaign once the banner slot exists.

**Worked if.** Asks posted that day ≥5× a normal day, and at least a third
get claimed.

### M4. The honest comparison page

**What.** `/compare`: a calm, factual page for the person typing "Nextdoor
alternative" — what Peoplearound is *for* (building things with named
teams), what it refuses to be (a feed of complaints, an ad surface), and the
privacy facts that are already true: no third-party ads, no data resale,
people pinned as community clusters rather than at home addresses, a data
export button, a published privacy page readable without an account.

**Why now.** It's the highest-intent search query in the category, and
every claim above is already shipped — this is writing, not building.

**First step.** Draft from [CONCEPT.md §7](CONCEPT.md) and the privacy
page. Name no competitor disparagingly; describe differences in kind.

**Effort.** One day.

**Worked if.** It ranks on page one for "{city} nextdoor alternative" in
the two live cities within a quarter; bounce rate under 60%.

### M5. The founding counter, in public — and ads that switch themselves off

**What.** Show "7 of 10 founding neighbors" on the public city page, and run
geo-fenced ads (a few dollars a day, one zip code) whose only creative is
that counter. When the tenth neighbor joins, the campaign stops.

**Why now.** Founding Neighbors is shipped and the scarcity is real, which
is why it passes the rule against manufactured scarcity — but today only
people already inside can see it. A budget that ends itself by design is also
the only kind of paid acquisition that can't quietly become a habit.

**First step.** Expose the count on `/city/[slug]` (it's a count, and
counts are what that page is allowed to show).

**Effort.** Half a day plus ad setup.

**Worked if.** Cost per founding neighbor under $8, and ≥60% of places that
reach 10 do so within 60 days (the metric already named in INCENTIVES §5).

### M6. A feed for newsletter editors

**What.** `/city/[slug]/feed.xml` (and `.json`): "what neighbors started
this week", "what needs hands this weekend" — titles, categories, links;
no names. Pitch it to the people who assemble local newsletters every week
and are short of items: neighborhood-association editors, Axios Local,
6AM City, church bulletins, library newsletters.

**Why now.** OUTREACH_TARGETS treats media as something to pitch once. An
editor who can paste a block every week without asking is a channel, not a
pitch.

**First step.** Ship the feed; hand-deliver it to two association editors
from the Tier 3 list.

**Effort.** One day.

**Worked if.** Three newsletters carry the block for four consecutive weeks.

### M7. The wall of thanks

**What.** Today an attestation is a click: "yes, this person did this." Add
one optional line at that moment — *say thanks in a sentence* — and, with
both people's explicit consent, publish a rotating handful on the logged-out
front page: *"Marcus rebuilt our gate in an afternoon. — Dee, Old Town."*

**Why now.** It is social proof that only this product can produce, because
the thanks was written for its own sake, to a specific person, about a
specific thing — not solicited as a testimonial.

**First step.** The optional thanks line on the confirmation step (a
nullable column on `attestations`), with a "may we show this on the front
page?" checkbox, off by default, asked of both parties.

**Effort.** Two days.

**Worked if.** Front-page → sign-up conversion rises measurably in an A/B
against the current hero.

### M8. The campus channel

**What.** Approach service-learning and volunteer offices — CU Boulder is
already an event source; UMKC and Rockhurst in Kansas City — with one offer:
students find *neighbor-led* projects near campus, and their confirmed
contributions export as a verified hours record (see D7).

**Why now.** Students need hours, have time, and graduate into being the
28-year-old who starts things. Offices need placements that aren't the same
four nonprofits. And the hours problem is one the product has already solved
by accident.

**First step.** One meeting with CU Boulder's Volunteer Resource Center,
carrying a mock-up of the transcript.

**Effort.** Outreach: days. Depends on D7 (about a week).

**Worked if.** One office lists Peoplearound among approved ways to log
hours.

### M9. Build in public, with the city pages as the receipts

**What.** A weekly operator's post — LinkedIn, the city subreddit where its
rules allow, Indie Hackers — in a fixed format: three numbers from the
public city page, one thing a neighbor built, one thing that didn't work.

**Why now.** A solo founder's most under-used asset is the story, and
`/city` already publishes the numbers, so nothing is claimed that a reader
can't check.

**First step.** Write four before posting the first, so the habit survives
week two.

**Effort.** An hour a week.

**Worked if.** Inbound partnership emails — the only metric that matters
for this one.

### M10. "What neighbors are building" — a quarterly data note for the press

**What.** A two-page note per city: what categories of project people
start (the `public_city_categories` view already aggregates this), what gets
finished, what stalls and why. Counts only.

**Why now.** Reporters cover data with a local angle far more readily than
they cover apps, and it positions the product as a source rather than a
supplicant. It also forces a quarterly look at the stall reasons, which is
worth doing anyway.

**First step.** Wait for ~50 projects in one city; below that the numbers
are anecdotes and saying so publicly would be worse than saying nothing.

**Effort.** A day per quarter.

**Worked if.** One local outlet cites it.

## Development

### D1. Move the database off the smallest tier

**What.** Move the Supabase project from the Free plan's Nano instance
(0.5 GB) to Micro (1 GB) or Small (2 GB).

**Why now — this is measured, not guessed.** Last night's work took ~400 ms
of structure out of every page (below), and then ran into a floor that isn't
in the code:

- The database host has **428 MB of RAM, 330 MB of it in swap, and 133
  million major page faults since boot.** Postgres, the API server, auth and
  realtime all live in that.
- The SQL is fast. `pg_stat_statements` shows no app query averaging over
  200 ms, and typical page queries run in 2–5 ms.
- But measured from *inside* the Vercel function, where a read normally
  takes ~30 ms, **about one read in twenty-five takes between 300 ms and
  2.4 s** — with a warm connection pool, and over a single HTTP/2 connection
  too, so it is not connection setup. It is the host paging itself back in.
- A page is as slow as its slowest read. At ~25 reads per page, most page
  views met at least one slow one. That is the skeleton you screenshotted.

**First step.** The organization is on Supabase's **Free plan**, and Nano is
the only size Free offers, so this is a plan change: Pro is $25/month and
includes Micro — double the memory. Small is about $5/month more on top
(the API quotes $0.0206/hour against Micro's $0.01344). Dashboard →
Organization → Billing, then Project Settings → Compute; it restarts the
database for a minute or two, so do it off-hours. The cost table in
[BUSINESS.md](BUSINESS.md) already assumes this line.

**Effort.** Ten minutes and $25–30/month.

**Worked if.** `.scratch/probe.mjs`-style repeated loads stop showing
multi-second outliers; swap in use stays near zero.

### D2. One-read pages

**What.** Continue what migration 0057 started. The frame (profile, rail
counts, inbox, map centre, communities) now arrives in one RPC instead of
twelve requests. Do the same for the two heaviest bodies: `/people` (~15
reads) and `/explore` (~12) each become one `security invoker` function
returning JSON.

**Why now.** Tail latency is paid per request, so request count is the
lever — true on any tier, and it keeps being true at 100× the traffic.

**First step.** `/people`, because it is the landing page.

**Effort.** Two days per page, mostly in proving the RPC returns exactly
what the separate queries did under each user's RLS (the 0057 check script
is the template).

**Worked if.** Reads per page view ≤6 on the top five pages.

### D3. A persistent shell

**What.** Move the signed-in pages into a route group with
`(app)/layout.tsx` owning the sidebar and top bar, and a `loading.tsx`
inside the group that replaces only the content column.

**Why now.** Every page renders its own `AppShell` today, so each
navigation tears down and rebuilds the rail, and the root loading skeleton
briefly replaces the *whole* screen, sidebar included. A layout survives
navigation: the chrome never blinks, its data isn't refetched per click,
and perceived speed improves more than any server change can.

**First step.** Move one page; confirm server actions that change rail
counts call `revalidatePath` on the layout.

**Effort.** Two days. Mechanical, but it touches every page's path.

**Worked if.** Clicking between rail items never redraws the rail.

### D4. A performance budget that fails the build, and real-user numbers

**What.** Two halves. (a) A test that renders each page against a recorded
fixture and fails if it makes more than N database requests — the
regression that matters here is someone adding a thirteenth read, and no
lint rule sees it. (b) `useReportWebVitals` posting LCP/INP/TTFB per route
to a small table, so "is it fast" is answered by visitors' phones rather
than by a laptop on good Wi-Fi.

**Why now.** Performance was fixed twice in a week by hand. The third time
should be a red CI run instead of a screenshot from you.

**Effort.** Three days.

**Worked if.** p75 LCP per route is on `/admin`, and a PR that adds a
sequential read fails.

### D5. An image pipeline

**What.** Avatars, project photos and event photos served at the size
they're shown, in a modern format, lazily below the fold — via `next/image`
with Supabase Storage as a remote pattern, or Storage's own transform URLs.
Cap dimensions client-side at upload in `PhotoField`.

**Why now.** On a feed page the images are the LCP and most of the bytes,
and [SCALING.md](SCALING.md) already names photo egress as the largest cost
line at scale — one change addresses both.

**Effort.** Two days.

**Worked if.** Feed page transfer size halves; LCP on `/people` under 2 s
on a mid-range phone.

### D6. Your plans, in the calendar you already use

**What.** A personal, tokenised `webcal://` feed: events you said "I'm in"
to, plus (optionally) everything your teams plan. Subscribe once in Google,
Apple or Outlook.

**Why now.** `/api/event-ics` already produces single events, so the
format work is done. A subscribed calendar is a retention surface that
needs no notification permission, no email, and no app open — the event
just *is* in the person's week.

**First step.** An unguessable per-user token (revocable from Settings),
because calendar clients can't send cookies.

**Effort.** Two days.

**Worked if.** ≥25% of people with an upcoming RSVP subscribe; no-show rate
falls.

### D7. A verified record of help given

**What.** "My contribution record": a printable page and PDF listing
confirmed contributions — what, when, for which project, attested by whom —
with a verification URL (`/verify/[code]`) that shows a third party the same
facts and nothing else.

**Why now.** The attestation model is the product's most distinctive asset
and currently has no exit from the app. Students, job-seekers,
court-ordered-service participants and corporate volunteers all need exactly
this document, and it is the keystone for M8, B2 and B7.

**First step.** The verify page, since it's what makes the PDF worth more
than a screenshot.

**Effort.** A week.

**Worked if.** Someone you've never met asks whether their organization
can accept it.

### D8. A smoke test that runs against production after every ship

**What.** `scripts/smoke.mjs`: create a throwaway account, sign in, load
every signed-in page, assert each streamed boundary resolved and no error
digest appears, check the frame shows the right name and counts, delete the
account. Wire it to the end of `npm run ship`.

**Why now.** Last night's refactor touched 19 pages at once, and the only
reason it was safe was an ad-hoc version of exactly this script. It should
not live in a scratch folder.

**Effort.** Half a day — **built overnight as `npm run smoke`**; it passes
against production. Chaining it onto `ship` is one line in
`scripts/ship.mjs`, left for you to decide because it creates and deletes
an account on every deploy.

**Worked if.** A broken deploy is known within two minutes of the push, by
the person who pushed it.

### D9. Search that finds things

**What.** Replace the `ILIKE '%term%'` lookups behind the top-bar search
with Postgres full-text search — a generated `tsvector` column and GIN
index on projects, events, offers and profiles — behind one RLS-respecting
function that returns ranked, typed results.

**Why now.** Substring match can't handle plurals, word order or typos,
and it scans. Search is how someone returning for a specific thing
navigates; when it misses, they conclude the thing isn't there.

**Effort.** Three days.

**Worked if.** Searches that end in a click rise from whatever they are now
(worth measuring first) to >50%.

### D10. Español

**What.** The interface in Spanish, chosen from the browser and switchable
in Settings; user-written content left as written.

**Why now.** The launch metros are not monolingual — Aurora is roughly a
third Hispanic, Kansas City's Westside and Northeast are substantially
Spanish-speaking — and the neighbors most under-served by existing local
networks are the ones an English-only product also leaves out. The first
network in a neighborhood to work in both languages tends to become *the*
network.

**First step.** Externalise strings on the five busiest pages; the root
layout's `lang="en"` is currently hard-coded.

**Effort.** Two weeks, plus a native speaker's review — not machine
translation alone, because the tone is the product.

**Worked if.** Spanish-browser sign-ups complete onboarding at the same
rate as English ones.

## Business development

*Sequencing note: [BUSINESS.md §2](BUSINESS.md) says no monetization before
10k users, and that stands. These are partnerships that bring density,
legitimacy or data **now**, and that turn into the B2B/B2G revenue lines
already planned later. Where money appears it's labelled.*

### B1. Social prescribing — with one clinic or agency on aging

**What.** A pilot in which clinicians, community health workers or an Area
Agency on Aging refer isolated patients to small, recurring, neighbor-led
activities on Peoplearound, and measure loneliness before and after with
the three-item UCLA scale (opt-in, in-app, two minutes).

**Why now.** Loneliness has been a named public-health priority since the
Surgeon General's 2023 advisory, and health systems, Medicare Advantage
plans and Older Americans Act programs have money earmarked for it with few
credible places to send people. If you have existing relationships anywhere
in health care, this is where they are worth the most. And the product's
native data — *who actually showed up, confirmed by someone else* — is the
outcome measure those buyers cannot get from a directory of programs.

**First step.** One federally qualified health center or AAA in one live
city. Ask for a 90-day pilot with 30 referrals, not a contract.

**Effort.** Relationship-heavy; the build is a referral code and a survey.

**Worked if.** Referred participants attend ≥2 activities in 60 days at a
rate above 40%. *Later revenue:* per-member-per-month from a plan, or grant
co-funding.

**Watch.** No clinical data enters the product — a referral code, never a
diagnosis — and participation must be invisible as such to other neighbors.

### B2. Employer volunteering

**What.** Companies that give staff paid volunteer time need two things
they struggle to find: local opportunities small enough for a team
afternoon, and proof the hours happened. Offer both — projects near the
office, and the verified record from D7.

**Why now.** It's the first buyer with an existing budget line for which
the product is already nearly complete. Mid-size regional employers are the
right first call; they're too small for enterprise CSR platforms and too
large for a spreadsheet.

**First step.** Two employers in Kansas City, one team day each, free, in
exchange for a named case study.

**Worked if.** A second team from the same company books without being
asked. *Later revenue:* $200–500/month per employer.

### B3. Destination marketing organizations — trade reach for a feed

**What.** Visit KC, Visit Boulder and their peers hold the best events data
in their cities and are funded by lodging taxes to make the city feel alive
— including to residents. Ask for a structured feed; offer attributed
listings and resident reach in return.

**Why now.** This is the actual fix for the crawler's remaining gap: you
pointed at visitkc.com on day one, and it's JavaScript-rendered with no
feed, so the crawler gets one event from it. Most DMO sites run on a
platform with an export the owner can switch on. A two-line email from a
partner beats any amount of scraping.

**First step.** Email Visit KC's digital/marketing lead. The crawler's own
error messages already say which sites need this ask: visitkc.com,
kclibrary.org, kcmo.gov.

**Worked if.** One official feed, and the listing count in that city triples.

### B4. Multifamily operators

**What.** Apartment operators budget for "resident experience" and measure
it in renewals. Offer a community per building or portfolio — free to
residents, forever — and later a paid manager's view: what residents
organized, participation trends, nothing about individuals.

**Why now.** It's a different buyer from the HOA boards in
OUTREACH_TARGETS: a business with a P&L, a retention problem, and staff
whose job is community. Density comes pre-assembled — 300 households at one
address.

**First step.** One mid-size local operator, one building, a lobby poster
and a first event hosted by the property's own community manager.

**Worked if.** ≥20% of units join in 60 days. *Later revenue:* $1–2 per
unit per month, the prevailing price in that market.

### B5. "The paint is on us" — local businesses sponsor materials, not attention

**What.** A hardware store, nursery or print shop pledges in-kind materials
to approved projects in its neighborhood — "$200 of paint for any block
project" — and is credited on the project page the same way a contributor
is: named, local, walkable, attached to something real.

**Why now.** It's the first revenue-adjacent idea that *strengthens* the
trust model rather than spending it. [BUSINESS.md §1a](BUSINESS.md)
describes sponsored placement in feeds; this is sponsorship of outcomes,
where the business's name sits next to a finished fence instead of above
someone's post. It also solves founders' most common blocker after
volunteers: small money.

**First step.** One store, one project, handled by hand.

**Worked if.** The store asks to do a second. *Later revenue:* a modest
listing fee once there's a waiting list of sponsors, never a cut of the
materials.

### B6. Run a city's neighborhood-grant program for them

**What.** Most cities run small neighborhood grants ($500–$5,000) and
administer them with PDFs and email. A Peoplearound project is already most
of an application: a described idea, a named team, a location, evidence of
support (stars), and afterwards a confirmed record that the work happened.
Offer the city a pipeline — apply from a project page, report from the
build log.

**Why now.** OUTREACH_TARGETS lists Aurora's grants as money *for*
Peoplearound projects. This makes the city the customer instead, and it's a
sharper B2G wedge than "engagement dashboard" because it replaces a process
staff already dislike.

**First step.** Read Aurora's and Kansas City's current application forms
and map every field to something a project page already holds.

**Worked if.** One grants officer says "could applicants just send us
that?" *Later revenue:* $300–1,000/month per program.

### B7. Universities, twice

**What.** Two doors on the same campus. *Service-learning offices* need
placements and verified hours (M8, D7). *Researchers* in public health,
sociology and urban planning need a field site for studying social
connection, and bring grant funding — NSF Smart and Connected Communities,
NIH/NIA for older adults — that can pay for evaluation you'd otherwise never
afford.

**Why now.** An independent evaluation is what B1's buyers will ask for in
year two. Start the relationship a year before you need the paper.

**First step.** One faculty conversation at CU Boulder or UMKC. Offer
aggregate, de-identified data and a willingness to randomize a feature.

**Watch.** Anything involving individuals' data goes through the
university's IRB and an explicit opt-in. The privacy page is a promise.

**Worked if.** A letter of support on a grant application within 12 months.

### B8. Service clubs

**What.** Rotary, Lions, Kiwanis, Optimist, Junior League: chapters in
every town, decades of project experience, a membership that is aging and
knows it, and tooling from another era. Offer each club a community page
and the thing they most want — younger neighbors who show up for a project
without first joining a club.

**Why now.** They aren't in OUTREACH_TARGETS, which covers sports bodies,
HOAs and civic institutions. A district governor's endorsement reaches
dozens of clubs at once, and these are people who finish what they start.

**First step.** Speak at one club lunch in Kansas City. They book speakers
weekly and are short of them.

**Worked if.** One club runs one project publicly and a non-member joins it.

### B9. Congregations and interfaith councils

**What.** Faith communities are the largest standing volunteer networks in
any American city — food drives, repairs, rides — organized on paper and
group chats. Offer a community kind for congregations, and approach through
the metro interfaith council rather than one congregation at a time.

**Why now.** They have what every new neighborhood lacks: people who
already turn up. Through a council, one conversation reaches many
traditions at once and keeps the platform visibly non-sectarian.

**Watch.** The product stays about *doing*: projects and help, open to any
neighbor. A congregation's page is a team, not a pulpit, and the existing
reporting tools apply to it as to anyone.

**First step.** Greater Kansas City Interfaith Council: one introduction,
one shared project — a coat drive is the right size for October.

**Worked if.** A project with teammates from two different congregations.

### B10. City captains

**What.** A part-time local organizer per city, with a written playbook, a
small monthly stipend, and a share of any local partnership they originate.
Their job is the unscalable part — walking into the library, hosting the
first three events, knowing which block has the person who starts things.

**Why now.** IDEAS_2026-09 has a whole section on tooling for *running many
cities alone*. Tooling doesn't walk into a library. Seven cities exist in
the database today and two of them have anything listed; the difference
between those and the rest is that someone local cared.

**First step.** Write the playbook from what actually worked in Kansas
City. Recruit the first captain from among the founding neighbors — they've
already shown the trait.

**Effort.** $200–400/month per city; fund the first from B5.

**Worked if.** A city with a captain reaches 50 active neighbors in a
quarter, and one without doesn't.

## What was deliberately left out

- **Anything that ranks people.** Still rejected, for the reasons in
  INCENTIVES §4.
- **Paid acquisition without a stopping rule.** M5 is the only ad spend
  here, and it ends itself.
- **A native app.** The PWA installs, works offline and sends push. A store
  listing via Trusted Web Activity is a cheap credibility win worth a day
  sometime, but it isn't top-thirty.
- **AI features for their own sake.** Idea-shaping earns its place. A
  chatbot wouldn't.
