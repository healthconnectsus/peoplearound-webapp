# Getting to three

How we land the first 3 organizations actually using the system. Written
2026-10-21 after the `OUTREACH_TARGETS.md` cold-email round produced zero
replies.

---

## 1. Diagnosis: what actually went wrong

Be precise about this, because "the strategy isn't working" is three different
problems and only two of them are real.

### a) The sample was too small to be evidence

At the playbook's own cadence — 10–15 sends/week, one follow-up — a few weeks
of sending is 20–50 emails. Cold outreach to associations and volunteers
replies at roughly 1–3%. The *expected* number of replies from 40 sends is
about one. Getting zero is a completely normal outcome of a tiny sample, not
proof the targets were wrong.

So: don't throw away the target list. It's fine. The channel is the problem.

### b) The channel has a low ceiling even at 10× volume

Cold email to volunteer club contacts and association inboxes is the worst
available channel for us, for reasons that don't improve with volume:

- **Deliverability.** A new domain sending cold mail with a link in it lands in
  spam. If outreach went out from a fresh `peoplearound` domain without SPF,
  DKIM and DMARC set up and warmed, a meaningful share was *never delivered* —
  you'd see zero replies and zero bounces, which looks exactly like rejection.
  **Check this before concluding anything.**
- **Volunteer inboxes aren't obligations.** A pickleball ambassador has no duty
  to answer email and often doesn't check the address in the directory.
- **Association HQs have gatekeepers and no incentive.** An unknown founder
  offering a free thing is indistinguishable from a vendor.

### c) The ask was too big, and the pitch was imaginary

Both real problems, and both fixable today.

| Current | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Open to a 20-minute call in the next couple of weeks?" | Costs them 20 minutes plus a decision, for an unknown return. |
| "Want me to set up your group's page as a demo so you can see it?" | Reads generous, but it's still an ask — saying yes creates an obligation to look, evaluate, and respond again. |
| Link to the app | They click, see an empty neighborhood with no people and no activity, and leave. The pitch requires them to *imagine* the populated version. Nobody does that work for a stranger. |

### d) We were pitching the one thing that already has free competitors

This is the strategic miss. Our templates lead with **group page + join flow +
events feed**. For the sports we targeted, that is a solved problem:

- **Pickleheads** gives pickleball organizers free groups, player lists,
  repeating sessions and one-tap RSVP. Their own copy: a shared home base for
  your regular players, posting notifies everyone, attach a group to a
  repeating session and it auto-invites members weekly.
- **Bounce** does court discovery and open-play listings.
- **Meetup** does groups + events + RSVP for everything else.

So a pickleball ambassador reading template A thinks: *I already have this, for
free.* No reply needed.

**What has no competitor** is the second half of our positioning — a neighbor
posts an idea, neighbors join the team, the thing gets built, and the people
who built it are **named and credited on it permanently**. Nobody serves that.
Pickleheads will never build a volunteer-recognition system for court
conversions. **Lead with the project and the credit, not the roster.**

### e) Timing

It's late October. Colorado outdoor leagues are wrapping up and organizers are
in off-season mode — which cuts both ways: fewer people thinking about rosters,
but the indoor-season transition is itself a coordination mess we can show up
in the middle of. Also note `MARKETING.md` still lists the World Cup afterglow
push as "time-sensitive, Aug–Sep 2026." That window closed; the doc is stale.

## 2. The reframe

Three changes. Everything in §3 follows from these.

### Change 1 — Redefine "organization"

You do not need three *institutions*. You need three **groups with one person
who can say yes and a live coordination pain**. A twelve-person Monday-night
doubles crew that currently runs on a group text is an organization for testing
purposes, and it's reachable in one evening instead of one quarter.

National associations are a Q2 play. Stop treating them as the path to three.

### Change 2 — Do the work first, then ask one closed question

Stop asking permission to build. **Build the page, then send it.**

> Hi — I'm Alexander, I live in Aurora. I went ahead and built your Monday
> glow league a page: {link}. Your course, your start time, the 14 names I
> could find. It's free and it's yours whether or not you ever talk to me.
>
> One question: is the roster right?

That costs them ten seconds and no obligation. It also solves (c) — they're
not looking at an empty app, they're looking at *their own group*. Expect a
reply rate several times anything cold email will do, because you've already
paid the cost you were asking them to pay.

Risk to manage: don't publish a page about someone's group publicly before
they've seen it. Build it unlisted/private, send the link, make it public only
once they say yes. Getting this wrong turns a warm opener into a creepy one.

### Change 3 — Go in person, and be willing to pay

- **In person beats every digital channel at this stage.** $5 gets you into an
  Aurora rec-center drop-in session. One evening of playing and listening
  yields more than a hundred emails, and the ask lands as a neighbor's offer
  rather than a vendor's.
- **Pay for pilot time.** A $150–250 design-partner stipend per group, for a
  4-week pilot and two feedback calls, is standard practice and converts
  roughly an order of magnitude better than free. Budget $750 for all three.
  Frame it honestly — you're buying their time and candor, not a testimonial
  or an endorsement, and say so in writing.

### Tier B — Meetup organizer arbitrage (best cold channel that exists)

Meetup organizers are the highest-intent prospects in the country for us,
because they have **already proven intent by paying money** for group +
events + RSVP. Third-party trackers put the standard organizer plan around
$45/month as of this month; the 2024 price increase caused real, documented
organizer backlash over both cost and lack of control.

The pitch writes itself: *you're paying ~$540/year for a roster and an events
list. Here's the same thing free, already built, with credit for the people who
actually do the work. Keep Meetup for discovery if you want — this isn't
either/or.*

Why this beats email: **Meetup has organizer messaging built in.** No domain
reputation, no spam filter, no stale directory address. The message arrives.

Named Denver/Aurora-area groups from the search: Colorado Volleyball and Coed
Sports Meetup, Pickup Sports (Adult League), Rocky Mountain Cycling Club,
Denver Cycling Club, Greater Denver Running Community, Roaming Colorado &
Beyond, Bicycle Aurora. Browse `meetup.com/find/us--co--aurora/` by category
to build the list to 30.

**Do 30 of these.** It's the one cold channel worth volume.

### Tier D — Be the organization yourself (the guaranteed yes)

This is not cheating and you should do it first. You need a *live* app to show
organizations #2 and #3, and right now the app is empty — which is the single
biggest reason a curious organizer bounces.

1. **Register a community group with the City of Aurora.** Free, 3+ people.
   Unlocks development notices, **free meeting rooms at libraries and fire
   stations**, and **eligibility for city neighborhood grants**. It also changes
   your identity in every later conversation from "startup founder" to
   "registered Aurora community group."
2. **Run one weekly thing.** A Saturday morning run from a fixed Aurora park, a
   Thursday cornhole night at Dry Dock. Ten people is enough. You are now
   organization #1, with real activity on real pages.
3. **Do one real project with credited volunteers.** Expo Park is a free 57-acre
   park with an 18-hole disc golf course — a tee-pad, signage, or cleanup
   project with the city, documented start to finish, with every contributor
   named on the page. This is the flagship asset from `MARKETING.md` §2, and it
   is also the only artifact that makes the attribution pitch legible instead
   of theoretical.

Being honest about the tradeoff: a founder-run group is weaker evidence than a
stranger adopting the product, so don't count it as one of the three if you can
avoid it. Count it as the demo that makes the other three possible.

## 5. Rewritten scripts

### The prebuilt-page opener (replaces template A)

> **Subject: built your {Monday glow league} a page — is the roster right?**
>
> Hi {name} — I'm Alexander, I live in Aurora, I was at {Centre Hills} on
> Monday.
>
> I build a free neighborhood app called Peoplearound, and I went ahead and
> set up a page for {league}: {link}. Your course, your 7:30 start, the
> {14} names I could find. It's unlisted until you say otherwise, and it's
> yours free whether or not we ever talk.
>
> One question: is the roster right?
>
> The part I actually care about is the other half — when your club does a
> {tee pad rebuild}, everyone who showed up gets named on that project's page
> permanently. No other tool does that. If it's useful I'd love your read on
> it, and I'll pay for an hour of your time.
>
> {signature}

Why it works: the work is already done, the ask is one closed question, the
differentiator is stated plainly, and the payment offer signals you value their
time rather than wanting a favor.

### The Meetup organizer DM (new, Tier B)

> Hi {name} — I run a free neighborhood app in Colorado and I'm looking for
> three groups to help me test it properly.
>
> You're paying Meetup somewhere around $45/month for a roster and an events
> list. I'll give you both free, permanently, plus the thing Meetup doesn't
> do: when your group builds or organizes something, every person who helped
> is credited on it by name, forever.
>
> Not asking you to leave Meetup — keep it for discovery. I'm offering $200
> for a 4-week trial and two half-hour calls telling me everything that's
> wrong with it. Interested?

### What to stop saying

- "Open to a 20-minute call in the next couple of weeks?" — too big, too vague.
- "a page for your club that neighbors can actually discover" — Pickleheads and
  Meetup already promise this; it reads as undifferentiated.
- "It's free and takes ten minutes to set up" — "ten minutes" is still ten
  minutes of *their* time. Take it to zero by building it yourself.
- Anything implying they should imagine the populated version. Show them theirs.

## 6. What to fix in the product first

Two things are probably costing more conversions than any outreach change:

1. **The empty state.** A curious organizer who clicks through to an empty
   neighborhood leaves and doesn't come back. Seed Aurora — real parks, real
   courses, a few real projects — before sending anyone a link.
2. **Make attribution the first thing visible.** It's our only uncontested
   differentiator and it's currently buried behind group/roster features that
   three competitors already give away. If the landing page leads with rosters,
   we lose on comparison; if it leads with "the people who built this are named
   here forever," there's nothing to compare to.
