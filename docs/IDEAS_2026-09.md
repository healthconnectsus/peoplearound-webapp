# Ideas — September 2026

Forty-two ideas generated against the live codebase, each judged twice: once
against the guardrails in [UX_SPEC §6](UX_SPEC.md#6-ux-anti-patterns-do-not-build)
and [INCENTIVES §1](INCENTIVES.md#1-principles-inherited-non-negotiable), and
once for whether it is actually buildable on this schema and useful to a
neighborhood of *five* people rather than five thousand.

They follow the shape of the owner's own requests — automatic local content,
tooling for one operator running many cities, seeding a city honestly, invite
mechanics, lifecycle email, and visibility into what people and communities
actually did.

Every entry names the files, tables and routes it touches, and states its own
failure mode. The risk line is not a disclaimer; several of these are one
careless implementation away from becoming the engagement machinery this
product exists to avoid, and the risk line is where that is said out loud.

Nothing here is scheduled. Move an entry to
[FEATURE_IDEAS](FEATURE_IDEAS.md) when it is chosen, and to the Rejected list
there if it is deliberately turned down.

## Running many cities alone

The operator lens. You asked for a city switcher and activity reports; these are the rest of that thought.

### /admin/health — a loop-stage board for every community

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** The only health signal today is the three-number strip on /admin/page.tsx (neighbors, projects, open flags) — global, not per-community, and all volume. There is no way to answer the two questions that actually matter to one operator running many cities: which neighborhood is genuinely working, and which one is about to go quiet. /admin/city/page.tsx lists residents and events but never says whether the loop ever closed there.

**How.** New migration 0050 adds a service-role-only, security-definer function `public.community_health()` (same grant pattern as `admin_activity` in 0047: revoke from public/anon/authenticated, grant to service_role). One row per `neighborhoods` row containing: real members from `community_members` (excluding accounts whose auth email ends @example.com, the same exclusion /api/digest already makes, and excluding `demo_residents` which are not accounts at all), distinct actors in the last 30 days (reuse the union-all `activity` CTE already written in 0047 over projects/events/offers/project_updates/contributions, plus `stars` and `rsvps`), `days_since_last_human_action`, and a loop stage computed as facts, not points: 0 empty → 1 two or more real members → 2 a project exists → 3 someone other than the owner starred or RSVP'd → 4 a `memberships` row reached status 'accepted' → 5 a `contributions` row reached status 'confirmed'. Store `stage_reached_at` so the board can say 'reached stage 4 on Aug 14, nothing since'. New page `src/app/admin/health/page.tsx` behind `requireAdministrator()` from `src/lib/admin.ts`, two sections — 'Working' (stage 4+ with action in 30d) and 'Stalling' (stage 3+ with nothing in 21d) — each row linking to the existing `/admin/activity?community=<id>` and `/admin/city?city=<id>`. Deliberately no 0–100 score: the stage plus the date it stopped is the honest statement.

**Why now.** With five people in a neighborhood, aggregate counts are noise; the only real question is whether anybody has yet been helped by anybody. Stage 5 in one city is worth more than 200 signups spread across six. This is the surface that tells the operator where to spend the one evening they have.

**What could go wrong.** At n=5 a single person's holiday flips a city from 'working' to 'stalling' — the board can produce false alarm and pull the operator toward whichever city is loudest rather than most winnable. It is also only as honest as its exclusions: if demo residents or the operator's own admin actions leak into the actor counts, every city reads healthier than it is, and the tool becomes vanity in a different costume. Also fragile: matching a community to a city still relies on string-normalizing `neighborhoods.city` against `event_cities.city_key`, the same brittle join `/admin/city/page.tsx` does today.

### /admin/funnel — signup → first action → first help, by weekly cohort per city

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** `/analytics` computes a funnel for one founder's own ideas, and `/admin/activity` reports what a chosen person posted. Nothing anywhere answers: of the nine people who signed up in this city, how many ever did one thing, and at which step do they stop? The operator cannot tell whether a city fails at arrival, at first star, or at the moment someone asks to join and nobody answers — and each of those needs a completely different fix.

**How.** Migration 0050 adds `public.signup_funnel(p_community uuid default null, p_city text default null, p_since date)`, service-role only, bucketing `profiles.created_at` into weekly cohorts and, per cohort, counting who ever: starred (`stars`), RSVP'd (`rsvps`), asked to join (`memberships` any status), was accepted (`memberships.status='accepted'`), logged a contribution (`contributions`), and had one confirmed (`contributions.status='confirmed'`), plus median hours from signup to first action and a count of accounts that never did anything. Split each cohort by origin using `profiles.invited_by` (0016) versus organic versus admin-invited, so the operator learns whether invited people convert better than walk-ins — the thing that decides whether invite mechanics are worth more effort. New page `src/app/admin/funnel/page.tsx`; lift the existing `FunnelBar` component out of `src/app/analytics/page.tsx` into `src/components/FunnelBar.tsx` so both surfaces use one bar.

**Why now.** The owner keeps building cold-start machinery (demo residents, welcome emails, city imports, clans) with no instrument that says which of them moved a real person one step. This makes each of those investments falsifiable at five-person scale, where a single cohort is readable by eye.

**What could go wrong.** It is a people-tracking screen, so it must stay aggregate: cohort counts only, drill-down solely through the existing `/admin/activity?user=`, and never a list of named inactive accounts — that list is one copy-paste from becoming a re-engagement mailing, which is exactly the engagement behaviour the guardrails forbid. Second risk: 'first star' is the easiest column to move, so the operator optimizes stars; the page should visually subordinate the top of the funnel to the last two columns (accepted onto a team, help confirmed), which are the only ones the mission counts.

### Worker heartbeats and 'it used to work' detection

*Effort: L*

**The problem.** Every background worker overwrites its own last state and keeps no history: `event_cities.last_status/last_error/last_import_count`, `city_event_sources.last_crawled_at/last_count/last_error`, `welcome_mail_jobs.status`. `/api/digest`, `/api/gardener` and `/api/push` record nothing at all — their result exists only in a Vercel log that scrolls away. So an import returning 'ok · 0 listings' for three weeks is visually identical to a healthy one, and a cron that stops firing entirely (rotated CRON_SECRET, a 401, a plan change) leaves no trace in the database whatsoever. This is a silent failure the operator can only discover by noticing a city's events page looks stale.

**How.** Migration 0050 adds `public.ops_runs (id, job text, scope text null, started_at, finished_at, status text, produced integer not null default 0, detail jsonb)` — service-role only, no RLS policies, same lockdown as `project_views` (0020). A small helper `src/lib/ops/record.ts` writes one row from each of `src/app/api/import-city-events/route.ts`, `crawl-city-events/route.ts`, `welcome-neighbors/route.ts`, `digest/route.ts` (produced = sent), `gardener/route.ts` (produced = nudges written) and `push/route.ts`. Then a new deadman cron `/api/ops-watch` (hourly, added to `vercel.json` beside the existing six) that raises only on 'was producing, now isn't': a ten-minute job with no successful run in the last hour; a city whose last three runs produced 0 while an earlier run produced more than 0; a calendar source with three consecutive zero crawls; `welcome_mail_jobs` rows stuck 'pending' past due_at + 2h. Alerts go to ALERT_EMAIL via the Resend call shape already used in `src/lib/welcome.ts` and `src/app/projects/flagActions.ts`, throttled by a `last_alerted_at` column so one broken job cannot send 24 emails a day; the same cron prunes `ops_runs` older than 90 days. Surface the last run per job as a strip on `/admin/health`.

**Why now.** Six crons already run every ten minutes on behalf of cities the operator is not looking at that week. Automation that fails silently is worse than no automation, because it removes the operator's reason to check. This is the cheapest thing on this list that prevents a city quietly starving.

**What could go wrong.** Alert fatigue is the failure mode: threshold it wrong and the operator mutes the channel, which is strictly worse than today. Note especially that a quiet week producing zero digest emails is correct behaviour by design and must never alert — the rule has to be relative to that job's own history, not to zero. And `ops_runs` is one more table to migrate and prune; if the pruning is forgotten it becomes the largest table in the database within a year.

### The Monday operator brief — one email to the operator, not to users

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** Every email the product sends goes to neighbors. The one person who has to decide where to spend their week gets nothing, and must log in and click through /admin, /admin/city and /admin/activity per city to reconstruct what happened. Decisions that need a human — flags, calendar sources discovered but left disabled, invitations nobody accepted — sit in tables with no clock on them.

**How.** New `src/app/api/ops-digest/route.ts` with the same `Bearer CRON_SECRET` gate as `/api/digest`, scheduled Mondays in `vercel.json`, sending one email to ALERT_EMAIL through the existing Resend pattern. Ordered as a to-do list, not a dashboard: (1) waiting on you — `project_flags` grouped by project, `city_event_sources` where enabled=false awaiting approval, invited accounts with no `last_sign_in_at` from `admin.auth.admin.listUsers`; (2) waiting on another neighbor — `memberships` pending over five days, `contributions` still 'logged' or accepted-but-unconfirmed over five days (the acknowledgment moment that never fired, which UX_SPEC §4.6 calls the emotional peak of the product), `offers` with kind='need' and `claimed_by is null` older than five days; (3) what moved — communities that changed loop stage this week from the `community_health()` function in idea 1; (4) workers — whatever `/api/ops-watch` flagged. Unlike the user digest it sends even in a dead week, because 'nothing happened anywhere' is precisely the fact the operator needs; cap it at roughly one screen with a link per item.

**Why now.** One person, many cities, no staff. The scarce resource is the operator's attention, and nothing currently allocates it. This is the cheapest possible allocator and it reuses the digest route's Resend plumbing almost verbatim.

**What could go wrong.** Sent to a single person with no unsubscribe, it can become a weekly guilt letter — a list of everything not done. Keep every line to an item with one clear action and one link, and drop sections that are empty rather than printing zeros. Bigger risk: section 2 tempts the operator to poke users directly ('your help was never confirmed'), which turns a neighborly product into a nagging one. The right action for a stalled contribution is usually to help the founder, and the email copy should say that rather than offering a one-click nudge.

### /admin/waiting — the cross-city queue of people nobody answered

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** When a real person takes the hardest step — asks to join, logs help, posts an ask for twenty minutes — the notification goes to exactly one other person, who may have gone quiet. If that founder never responds, the moment dies invisibly. At five people per neighborhood this single unanswered act is usually the whole difference between a live community and an empty room, and no screen in the product shows it.

**How.** New page `src/app/admin/waiting/page.tsx` behind `requireAdministrator()`, one list across all cities, oldest first, each row naming the city and the age of the wait: `memberships` where status='pending' and created_at older than N days; `contributions` where status='logged' (founder never accepted) or accepted with fewer than one row in `attestations` (co-attestation never happened, so the confirmation moment cannot fire); `offers` with kind='need', claimed_by null, older than its `when_text` implies; a first project by a first-time founder with zero rows in `stars` after seven days. Optionally a DM signal from `conversations`/`messages`/`conversation_participants.last_read_at` — and if so, metadata only: participants and timestamps, never selecting `messages.body`, with the page stating that plainly. Each row offers exactly three human actions and no automation: open the project, open `/admin/activity?user=<founder>`, or start a DM as yourself. Age thresholds live in one constant so they can be tuned per city size.

**Why now.** Every cold-start mechanic already shipped (demo residents, welcome emails, imported events, clans) works on getting people to act. This is the first tool aimed at what happens after they act, which is where a five-person neighborhood actually dies.

**What could go wrong.** This is the closest the product comes to surveillance, and it earns that only by staying metadata-only — no message bodies, no inactive-user list, no bulk action. If it ever grows an automated nudge it becomes the engagement machinery the guardrails reject. There is also a real creepiness cost: an operator DMing a stranger about their five-day-old join request reads as being watched unless the message is genuinely personal and rare. And attesting a contribution the operator did not witness would corrupt the trust core — the page must never offer that button.

### Batch city actions with a dry run and an audit log

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** Every operator action is one form for one city: `populateEventsNow` takes a single cityId, `generateDemoResidents` a single cityId, `saveCalendarSource` a single URL for a single city — each with its own redirect round-trip through /admin. Running eight cities is eight passes, so in practice the operator only ever touches the city they are already thinking about, and the others drift. Nothing records what was done, so a mistake made at 1am cannot be reconstructed the next morning.

**How.** New `src/app/admin/BatchTools.tsx` and `src/app/admin/batchActions.ts` beside the existing `GrowthTools.tsx`/`cityActions.ts`: a checkbox list of `event_cities` (plus select-all-enabled) and a small set of safe operations — queue imports (set `next_run_at = now()` on the selected ids and let the existing leased queue drain them, exactly as the all-cities branch of `populateEventsNow` already does), toggle `enabled` for weekly imports, add one calendar source URL across several cities (upsert per city into `city_event_sources` on the existing (city_id,url) conflict target), seed demo residents up to N via the existing `seed_demo_residents` RPC, or append a state/country suffix to `search_location`. Every batch renders a dry-run confirmation naming each affected city before writing, and each applied action writes a row to a new `admin_audit (id, actor uuid, action text, scope jsonb, created_at)` table from migration 0050, shown as 'last touched by you on …' on `/admin/health`. Explicitly excluded from batching: anything that emails or messages real people — `inviteRealResident` stays one at a time by design.

**Why now.** The owner just asked for a city switcher, which is the same complaint in a different shape: the console assumes one city at a time and the operator has many. Batching the config-and-content operations is what makes 'ten cities' a plausible workload for one person.

**What could go wrong.** Batch amplifies mistakes: one wrong `search_location` applied to ten cities pulls another city's events into ten neighborhood feeds, and residents see it before the operator does. The dry run and the audit log are mitigation, not prevention. The stronger discipline is the exclusion list — the day someone adds 'invite everyone' or 'message all cities' to this panel, a neighborly product becomes a spam cannon, and the demo-resident seeder in particular must stay capped so a batch cannot flood a city with example profiles that outnumber its real people.

### 'What a stranger sees today' — a per-city newcomer preview

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** Counts do not convey emptiness. The operator can read that Aurora has four members and two events and still have no idea that a person arriving there today lands on a feed with nothing human in it, an already-retired founding banner, and four labelled example profiles as the most prominent content on the page. The failure modes that kill first impressions — imported events that are all in the wrong city, demo residents outnumbering real content — are invisible in every table the console shows.

**How.** New `src/app/admin/city/preview/page.tsx` (linked from `/admin/city`) that, for a chosen city, renders the newcomer's-eye summary from anon-safe sources only: the public views added in migration 0043 that already back `/city/[slug]`, upcoming rows from `city_events` for that `city_id`, open projects and `offers` with kind='need' for the communities whose normalized `neighborhoods.city` matches the city_key, the founding-banner state (n of 10 from `community_members` join order), and the `demo_residents` count. Headline it with the honest sentence — 'a person arriving in Aurora today sees 0 neighbor projects, 2 imported events, 4 example profiles' — and put the real-content versus example-content ratio next to each other deliberately. Implemented as a read-only render of anon-safe data, never a session impersonation and never a service-role read of private rows.

**Why now.** Cold start is a first-impression problem, and the operator is the only person who will ever look at ten cities' first impressions. This is the cheapest way to feel the empty room before a stranger does, and it directly checks whether the demo-resident and event-import machinery is actually producing something worth arriving to.

**What could go wrong.** Seeing the empty room creates pressure to fill it, and the dishonest fill is right there — more demo residents, more imported listings dressed as local life. Showing the example-to-real ratio on the same screen is the guard, but it is a guard the operator has to respect. Second risk: any 'view as' tooling drifts toward impersonation; this must stay a render of anon-safe views, or it becomes a way to read private neighborhood content from an admin page. Scope creep is likely too — reusing the live feed components rather than a summary turns this from a session's work into a day's.

## Honest content supply

More real local substance a new city can have on day one, without pretending.

### OpenStreetMap places-to-meet import (Overpass)

*Effort: L*

**The problem.** A five-person neighborhood cannot start a repair café, a garden, or a walking group because nobody knows what rooms and spaces exist. Every playbook's first step is "find a room / find a lot" and the app currently answers with nothing. Ticketmaster and the calendar crawler supply *events*; nothing supplies *places*.

**How.** New migration 0050 creating `public.city_places` (city_id → event_cities, osm_type, osm_id, kind, name, lat, lng, source_url = openstreetmap.org/{type}/{id}, last_seen_at, expires_at), RLS reusing the existing `public.can_read_city_events(city_id)` helper, `grant select ... to authenticated`, service-role writes only — the exact shape 0045 already established. New pure normalizer `src/lib/city-events/places.ts` (`overpassPlaces(payload, now)`) mirroring `normalize.ts` conventions: no fetches, no clients, no inferred data. Query Overpass at `https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter?data=...` for `nwr(around:8000,lat,lng)` matching `amenity=library|community_centre|townhall|marketplace|public_bookcase`, `leisure=park|garden|pitch`, `landuse=allotments`. Because Overpass is a *fixed provider host*, call it through the `apiJson()` path in `src/lib/city-events/importer.ts` (30s timeout, no-store) — never through `safe-fetch.ts`, which exists for admin-approved discovered URLs. Add it as a fourth isolated try/catch step inside `populateCity()` so an Overpass outage cannot erase Ticketmaster results, gated by a new `places_checked_at` column refreshed at most every 30 days (same pattern as `sources_checked_at`), keeping well inside Overpass's volunteer fair-use policy. Bound the query with an explicit `out ... 300;` so the response stays small. Surface it in the wizard's where-step, on `/playbooks`, and as map pins via `src/lib/mapPins.ts`. Attribution line "© OpenStreetMap contributors" — the app already renders OSM tiles, so the obligation is familiar.

**Why now.** It is keyless, global, free, and true on day one in every city the owner will ever open, unlike Ticketmaster (weak outside ticketed entertainment) and unlike calendar crawling (needs a per-city human approval). And it supplies the one thing five people actually lack: somewhere to meet. `amenity=public_bookcase` is the Little Free Library tag, `leisure=garden` the community-garden one — the two playbooks most likely to be started are the two OSM covers best.

**What could go wrong.** OSM is uneven and sometimes stale: a "community centre" may have closed, a park may be fenced off. If the app asserts facts it cannot check, it looks careless in exactly the place it is asking for trust. Mitigate by never stating hours, access, or bookability, always linking to the OSM object so a neighbor can see and correct the source, and wording it as "mapped as" rather than "available". Second risk: a place listing implies an endorsement or a booking relationship that does not exist.

### "Going together" on an imported listing

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** `city_events` has a select grant and nothing else — an imported listing is a link out of the product. A neighbor reads that there is a free concert Thursday and still goes alone, or doesn't go. The import gave the city substance but no reason to meet, which is the whole mission.

**How.** Migration adding `public.city_event_going` (city_event_id → city_events on delete cascade, user_id → profiles, note text check length ≤ 140, created_at, primary key (city_event_id, user_id)). RLS: insert/select/delete gated by `exists (select 1 from public.city_events e where e.id = city_event_id and public.can_read_city_events(e.city_id))`, delete restricted to `user_id = auth.uid()`. Because listings are upserted on the stable `(city_id, provider, external_id)` key, the row id survives weekly refreshes, so a going signal persists across imports; add a sweep for rows whose listing is past `expires_at`. `src/app/events/CityEvents.tsx` gains "I'm thinking of going" plus the neighbors already going, shown as names/avatars — "Maria and 2 others" — never as a count badge. The `note` is deliberately a meeting point, not a comment: placeholder "I'll be at the north gate at 6:45". Ordering in the list stays `event_date, provider` exactly as it is today; never by going-count.

**Why now.** Five neighbors will not all be free for a Saturday build, but three of them can go to the same free thing that already exists. It is the lowest-stakes on-ramp in the product, it runs on content the city already has on day one, and it reuses the RSVP asymmetry the docs already blessed — presence rewardable, absence never recorded.

**What could go wrong.** Two real ones. (1) It can become a shallow attendance surface that substitutes for building — the ROADMAP's anti-metric. Keep it out of `lib/impact.ts`, badges, `/analytics`, milestones and `/recap` entirely, and send at most one notification per person per listing. (2) Safety: "I will be at this place at this time" is disclosure. Restrict visibility to signed-in same-city neighbors via the existing helper, and say so plainly at the moment of posting, the way the offers pin-blunting copy does.

### Calendar feed sniffer — turn dead discovery candidates into live sources

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** CITY_EVENT_IMPORTS admits the gap: visitkc.com and kclibrary.org publish no structured data and "need an .ics feed URL or a small adapter". Discovery saves ten candidate URLs per city; the admin must then guess the format and hand-enter an exact final URL in `saveCalendarSource`. That manual step is the operator's per-city bottleneck, so most discovered candidates stay disabled forever.

**How.** New `src/lib/city-events/sniff.ts`. Given one candidate the admin explicitly clicks "Check" on, `safeGet` it once and look for, in order: `<link rel="alternate" type="text/calendar">`; anchors ending `.ics` or containing `ical=1`; a `calendar.google.com/calendar/embed?src=<id>` iframe, derived to `https://calendar.google.com/calendar/ical/<encoded id>/public/basic.ics`; a Springshare LibCal `/calendar/<name>` path, derived to the same path with `?cid=&ical=1` (most US public libraries run LibCal, which is why this one pattern unlocks library calendars nationally); otherwise the presence of JSON-LD `Event`. Persist to new `city_event_sources` columns `sniffed_format`, `sniffed_feed_url`, `sniffed_at`. `src/app/admin/CalendarSources.tsx` gains a "Use this feed" button that fills the existing form, plus a preview of the first five parsed events (run through `icsEvents`/`jsonLdEvents` without writing) before enabling. Derived URLs still pass `sourceUrl()` and `safeGet`'s DNS pinning and robots check — nothing changes about the fetch boundary.

**Why now.** Discovery already proposes; approval is already the human gate. This does not weaken that boundary — it just stops the approval step from requiring the operator to read someone's HTML by hand. One operator running many cities converts a list of dead candidates into real local content per click instead of per hour.

**What could go wrong.** A derived Google Calendar ICS may be an organization's internal calendar that was made public by accident, or a feed of something irrelevant (staff shifts, room bookings) that would flood a city with noise. The five-event preview before enabling is the mitigation and it must be mandatory, not optional. Also LibCal and Google URL shapes change; when a derived URL 404s the source should report it plainly in admin rather than silently producing zero events, which is how the first Kansas City search failure hid itself.

### Local playbook facts

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** `src/lib/playbooks.ts` is a static array of eight generic entries. "Find out who owns the lot" and "a room with tables and power" are advice, not next steps. A person who opens `/playbooks` in a cold city gets the same words as everyone in every city, which is exactly the moment the product could be specific and is not.

**How.** Migration adding `public.city_playbook_facts` (city_id, playbook_slug, fact_kind, label, source_url, source_name, last_seen_at, unique(city_id, playbook_slug, source_url)), same RLS helper. Populated by the same leased city run: OSM `amenity=library`/`community_centre` rows become repair-café and skill-swap room facts; `leisure=garden` + `landuse=allotments` become community-garden facts; `amenity=public_bookcase` becomes little-free-pantry facts; a discovered municipal page matching "cleanup"/"bulk waste" becomes the block-cleanup supplies fact. Add `localFactsFor(cityId, slug)` to `src/lib/playbooks.ts` and render in `src/components/PlaybookList.tsx` and `/playbooks` as a bounded "In your city" block under the existing First step line: three facts max, each a link, each with a checked-on date. A playbook with no facts renders exactly as it does today — the generic version — never an invented local specific.

**Why now.** The playbooks doc says a vague ask is the top reason an idea gathers nobody; the same is true of a vague first step. "Nine libraries near you have meeting rooms, here are four with links" is the difference between opening the wizard and freezing versus booking something. It also gives the OSM import a job on a page people already visit.

**What could go wrong.** Stale facts are worse than no facts here, because this is the surface where the product is asking someone to act on what it says. Enforce: every fact carries a source link and a visible "checked <date>", facts expire like listings do, and the copy links out rather than asserting availability. Second risk: over-stuffing turns a calm page into a directory — hard-cap the block and keep the human first step first.

### Public-meeting import as "decisions being made near you"

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** The most consequential genuinely local content that exists — the council meeting about the vacant lot, the parks board hearing, the school board vote — is free, published, and completely absent. Ticketmaster gives a city concerts; nothing gives it stakes. A neighborhood that only shows entertainment does not feel like a place where anything is decided.

**How.** Extend the `city_events` provider check constraint to include `'civic'` (the 0049 migration already shows the drop-constraint/add-constraint pattern). Add `legistar_client text` and `civic_enabled boolean default false` to `event_cities`, editable in `src/app/admin/EventImports.tsx` alongside the existing search-location field. New `src/lib/city-events/civic.ts` with a pure `legistarEvents(payload, now)` normalizer over `https://webapi.legistar.com/v1/{client}/events` — free, no key, and schema-consistent across the several hundred US cities on Legistar, which is why one adapter is worth writing where a generic Socrata adapter was correctly rejected. Cities on Granicus/CivicPlus instead publish ICS and need no new code at all: they go through the existing `city_event_sources` crawler with `format='ics'`. Render as its own subsection in `src/app/events/CityEvents.tsx` — body name, date, agenda link — visually separate from "Around your city".

**Why now.** It pairs directly with "start something from this": a zoning item about a vacant lot is literally the origin story of the community-garden playbook. And it is the content most likely to make a person tell a neighbor about the app, which is the only distribution a cold-start neighborhood has.

**What could go wrong.** Politics. Council content can pull a warm product toward argument, which is the opposite of the mission. Keep it listing-only — no positions, no commentary surface, no counts of who agrees — link to the official agenda, and make it opt-out per city in admin, because not every city or every operator wants it. Second risk: Legistar client IDs must be found per city by hand, so this is one more manual field; it should degrade to "not configured" silently rather than erroring, and cities without Legistar should just use the ICS path.

### "Start something from this" on an imported listing

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** Imported listings are a terminal surface: an outbound link and nothing else. There is no route from the content that exists on day one into the loop the product actually cares about — projects, small help, neighbor-led events. The import solves the empty-room problem and then leaves the room a reading room.

**How.** Two secondary links on each card in `src/app/events/CityEvents.tsx`: `/asks?from=ce&ce=<id>` and `/projects/new?from=ce&ce=<id>`. Both read the id server-side through the caller's own session so RLS decides whether they may see it — the same discipline `/api/export-my-data` uses — and prefill. For the project wizard (`src/app/projects/new`), seed the description with an editable sentence naming the listing and its date, drop the date into the existing `when_text` field from migration 0037, and pass the venue to the map-pin step. For `/asks`, prefill a short need ("anyone want to walk over together") with an honest time estimate preselected. Everything stays editable, exactly like the `?playbook=<slug>` prefill already does. The created project stores no foreign key to the listing and makes no claim on it — at most a source line in the description the person can delete.

**Why now.** It is the cheapest idea here — one query and two links — and it is the one that converts automatic supply into human loop. Without it, every other import idea on this list increases the amount of stuff to read.

**What could go wrong.** It can manufacture thin projects that exist only to attend something. An event is not a project, and a page whose whole arc ends Thursday will read as dead by Friday — near the line the docs draw around never implying failure. Mitigate by ordering the options honestly: "go together" (the going signal) first, `/asks` second, "start something that outlasts it" third and worded that way, so starting a project is the deliberate choice rather than the default.

### Public source ledger + cold-room report

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** As auto-supply grows, "how do I know what here is real" becomes the top trust question, and today the answer is one line of small grey text per card. Meanwhile the operator has no view of which cities are actually cold — `/admin` shows per-city import status, but not which cities have real neighbors staring at an empty page.

**How.** Two parts. (1) A single shared provenance component `src/components/ImportedSource.tsx` used everywhere imported content lands — `CityEvents.tsx`, the welcome email in `src/lib/welcome.ts`, `/city/[slug]` — reading "Listing from <source> · not posted by a neighbor", so the distinction can never drift between surfaces. Plus a public `/city/[slug]/sources` page listing every enabled source, what it is, when it was last read, and how many listings it produced, backed by an anon-safe view in the style of migration 0043 so it is readable signed out. (2) An admin "cold rooms" panel in `src/app/admin/page.tsx`: cities where `city_events` is empty or every source is failing, ranked by how many *real* verified neighbor accounts are there (not demo residents), so the operator's hour goes where a person is currently being disappointed. Add an explicit assertion in the test suite that no `city_events`/`city_places` row can reach `lib/impact.ts`, `lib/milestones.ts`, `/recap`, `/analytics`, or badge derivation.

**Why now.** The demo-residents decision already set the precedent: labelling is cheap now and unrecoverable later. A public source page is also the best outreach artifact the owner has — a librarian who sees their calendar already listed, read politely and attributed, is a much warmer first conversation than a cold email.

**What could go wrong.** Naming a library or tourism board as a "source" can read as a claimed partnership. The page must say plainly that these are publicly published calendars read within robots.txt, not partners, and carry a one-click admin removal. Expect at least one site owner to ask to be removed; without a stated policy that becomes an inbox problem rather than a form. Also, per-source listing counts are counts about data, not people — keep them there and never let a source page become a ranking of neighborhoods.

## Why someone opens it again

Return triggers that come from mattering rather than from streaks or bait.

### Events reach the people who starred

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** The star is the product's designed first action, and it is a dead end. `notif_star` (0025) tells the founder; `notif_event` fans out only to accepted `memberships`. Someone who starred four ideas in week one is never told when any of them gets a date. The single most honest interruption the product has — "the thing you wanted to exist is happening Saturday" — is not wired.

**How.** Migration 0050: drop/re-add `notifications_kind_check` to admit `starred_event` and `reminder`. Add trigger `notif_event_stargazers` AFTER INSERT on `events`: insert one notification per distinct `stars.user_id` for that `project_id`, excluding `projects.owner_id`, excluding accepted `memberships` (already covered by `notif_event`), excluding `auth.uid()`; href `/projects/<id>`. Then the T-24h pass: `events.reminded_at` column + SQL function `events_starting_soon()` returning events 20–28h out joined to their `rsvps`; a new route `src/app/api/event-reminders/route.ts` (same `Bearer CRON_SECRET` gate as `/api/digest`), hourly in `vercel.json`, writes one `reminder` notification per (event, user) and stamps `reminded_at` so it can never double-send. Push is free: `pending_pushes` (0032) drains any unread notification within ten minutes.

**Why now.** With five neighbors, a star is often the only interaction a project ever gets, and it currently generates exactly one notification — to someone else. This turns the cheapest action in the app into a reason to come back, and it needs no additional users to work.

**What could go wrong.** `notifications` is both the bell and the push queue, so a project posting three events in a week buzzes everyone who ever starred it three times. A star means "I want this to exist", not "subscribe me", so the trigger needs a NOT EXISTS guard (at most one `starred_event` per user per project per 7 days), the body must say "you starred this" so the reason is legible, and Settings needs a third opt-out beside `digest_opt_out` / `push_opt_out`.

### The morning-after wrap-up

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** An event happens and the software goes silent. `src/app/projects/[id]/page.tsx` shows past events at 60% opacity with a passive line ("Did neighbors help make this happen?") that only appears if someone happens to visit. The post-event beat — it happened, who came, what's next — is where mattering is actually felt, and nothing brings anyone back to it.

**How.** Migration adds `events.wrapped_at` and a function `past_events_awaiting_wrapup()`. New daily cron `src/app/api/after-event/route.ts` (vercel.json, `Bearer CRON_SECRET`) writes ONE notification to the founder and any `memberships.role='co_organizer'`: "Yesterday's build day happened — who was there?", href `/projects/<id>?wrap=<eventId>`. That link opens a wrap-up block in the project page listing that event's `rsvps` with a "they were there" control per person, plus a prefilled update composer (`project_updates`, 0021) and the existing `eventForm` prefilled with the same weekday/time/place for "plan the next one". Marking someone present deliberately writes NO contribution — the 0005 insert policy keeps `contributor_id = auth.uid()`, and inventing deeds for people is the gameable path. It writes that attendee a notification: "Maria says you were at X — log what you did?", linking to the contribution composer with `type=presence` prefilled. The existing accept + attest pipeline then produces the confirmed moment and the 🙋 Showed Up badge in `src/lib/badges.ts`.

**Why now.** Five people at one Saturday event is already a complete neighborhood story. This converts one real-world hour into the acknowledgment peak, several genuine notifications, and a next date — the highest-yield loop the product has, and it scales down, not up.

**What could go wrong.** For a host who ran something casual, a wrap-up prompt is a chore, and a host who ignores it leaves attendees with a silence they can now feel. `wrapped_at` must be stamped by any of the three buttons so the prompt fires exactly once and never nags. The screen must never show an attendance count or an empty "0 came" state — that is the failure screen UX_SPEC §6 forbids, wearing a different hat.

### A claimed ask gets a time

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** "I'll help" is the yes, and then the software stops. `claimAsk` in `src/app/asks/askActions.ts` sets `claimed_by`/`claimed_at`, and the 0033 claim notification links to a board — not to a person, not to a time. A yes with no time is where the sofa never actually gets moved, and neither side has a reason to open the app again.

**How.** Migration adds `offers.agreed_at timestamptz` and `offers.agreed_place text`, plus a third UPDATE policy scoped to claimed rows where `auth.uid() in (user_id, claimed_by)` and a BEFORE UPDATE trigger rejecting changes to `title`/`description`/`minutes` from the non-poster — 0034 exists for exactly this class of hole. After `claimAsk`, show the helper a one-line "when?" (quick picks derived from `when_text`, plus a datetime input) and an optional link into the DM the existing `start_conversation(p_other)` RPC (0044) already creates — offered, never forced. Once `agreed_at` is set it renders on the ask card in `src/components/AsksSection.tsx` and in the feed strip via `openAsks()` in `src/lib/asks.ts`, yields an .ics by widening `src/app/api/event-ics/route.ts` to accept `?ask=<id>` (read through the caller's session so RLS decides), and a small morning-of cron pings both sides.

**Why now.** The ask board is the lowest-activation surface in the product and the only one that needs exactly two people. Turning a claim into an appointment is the shortest path from a tap to two humans standing in the same room, which is the entire point.

**What could go wrong.** A pinned time makes stepping back feel like breaking a promise — precisely what the "either side can step back" rule in UX_SPEC exists to prevent. `reopenAsk` must stay a first-class button sitting beside the agreed time, and the morning-of message must be a reminder, never an accountability notice. Widening the .ics route is a real exposure surface: it must never touch the service role.

### Boards that know what day it is

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** `openAsks()` returns the four newest open `need` rows regardless of age, and `when_text` is free text, so "Saturday morning" rides the top of `/explore` and `/people` until someone deletes it. A board of stale asks makes a small neighborhood read as abandoned — the opposite of the proof-of-life the feed is for — and nobody is ever asked whether the need still stands.

**How.** Two halves. (a) Freshness in `src/lib/asks.ts`: `openAsks` filters to `created_at > now() - interval '10 days'` OR a new `offers.renewed_at`, and the strip in `src/app/explore/page.tsx` and `src/app/people/page.tsx` shows "asked 2 days ago". Older open asks stay on the full board in `AsksSection.tsx` under a quiet "Older" heading — hidden from the strip, never deleted, because in a five-person neighborhood removing rows makes the room emptier. (b) A "still needed?" pass: a branch inside the existing Saturday `/api/digest` run (no new cron) writes one notification to the poster of any unclaimed ask 5+ days old, with two one-tap server actions in `askActions.ts` — "still needed" (sets `renewed_at = now()`, back to the strip) and "sorted, thanks" (the existing `closeAsk` delete path, which already treats a small ask as having no history worth keeping).

**Why now.** With five neighbors the board has maybe three rows, and whether those three look alive IS the impression the product makes on a week-two visit. This is the cheapest thing on this list that changes how the place feels.

**What could go wrong.** Pinging someone about an unanswered ask reads as "nobody helped you" unless the copy is careful. It must ask about the need and never about the response ("Do you still need a hand with the sofa?"), offer widening (post it to the city, or turn it into a small project) rather than implying the ask failed, and fire once per ask, ever. The 10-day cutoff is also a guess — it should be tunable, because a tool-borrowing ask is not a Saturday ask.

### Your offer meets the ask it was made for

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** An offer of kind `offer` (a skill) is posted once and then sits on a board nobody reopens. Meanwhile a neighbor posts a `need` for exactly that. Both rows are in the same `offers` table, in the same `neighborhood_id`, and the software never introduces them — the one piece of matchmaking the data plainly supports.

**How.** Trigger on `offers` AFTER INSERT where `kind = 'need'`: match `new.title || ' ' || new.description` against open (`claimed_by is null`) `kind = 'offer'` rows with the same `neighborhood_id`, using `to_tsvector('simple', title || ' ' || description) @@ websearch_to_tsquery(...)` with a GIN index added in the same migration. Take at most 3 and call `public.notify(offerer, 'match', '🙌 Someone nearby needs a hand with X — you offered Y', '/people#asks')`, which needs the `notifications_kind_check` widening from idea 1. Hard caps live in the trigger: never match the asker's own offers, and skip anyone who already received a `match` notification in the last 24h (NOT EXISTS on `notifications`). Deliberately need→offer only, never project→person: matching people to people is fuzzier and edges toward the "meet anyone" line CONCEPT §9 forbids.

**Why now.** Cold start means the few people present each carry one or two specific things they can do. The entire value of a five-person network is that the software knows those two facts and can point them at each other once, at the right moment.

**What could go wrong.** A bad match is worse than silence: it teaches people the bell lies, and because `pending_pushes` drains any unread notification, a false match also becomes a phone buzz. Keyword matching on free text will produce embarrassing pairings ("moving boxes" ↔ "I can move furniture" is right; "help with my dog" ↔ "I walk a lot" is not). Ship with the daily cap, an `/admin` sample of what it matched last week (`src/app/admin/activity` already has the reporting grammar), and an env kill switch.

### The digest leads with the thing you touched

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** `src/app/api/digest/route.ts` builds one email per neighborhood — new ideas, upcoming events, unread count — so the person who starred three things and joined nothing gets a newsletter about strangers. That cohort is the largest and most fragile one the product produces, because onboarding deliberately targets a star as the first action, and the only channel that still reaches them is telling them about other people.

**How.** Inside the existing route, before the send loop, add two grouped queries alongside the `unreadBy` map: all `stars` (project_id, user_id) and all accepted `memberships` + `rsvps` for the opted-in profile set, grouped in memory — the same N+1-avoiding shape the route already uses for unread counts. If a recipient has ≥1 star, no accepted membership and no RSVP, lead their email with their starred project that has the nearest upcoming `events` row, else its newest `project_updates` row (0021), subject "Something you starred is happening" / "…moved". Everyone else keeps today's email. The quiet-week promise is untouched: if that project has no event and no update, there is nothing true to say and the branch falls through to the existing skip.

**Why now.** One operator seeding several cold neighborhoods cannot hand-hold each newcomer, and this is the only channel that reaches someone who has already stopped opening the app. It costs one route file and no schema.

**What could go wrong.** It sits one sentence away from "you haven't done anything yet", which is the guilt mechanic UX_SPEC §6 forbids. The copy must be entirely about the project moving and never about the reader's inactivity, must never count their stars back at them, and must not feature the same project two weeks running — either track the last-featured project id per user or require the featured item to be newer than the last send.

### The neighbor you brought was just confirmed

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** Inviting is the highest-leverage act during a cold start, the currency is already recorded (`profiles.invited_by`, 0016), and the inviter never hears another thing after the sign-up. INCENTIVES §3 item 4 lists this as design-approved and unbuilt: the moment an invite turns into something real is invisible to the person who made it happen.

**How.** Extend `notif_confirmed` (0025), or add a sibling trigger on `contributions` when `status` flips to `confirmed`: look up `profiles.invited_by` for `new.contributor_id`, and when this is that person's FIRST confirmed contribution (count on `contributions` where `contributor_id = new.contributor_id and status = 'confirmed'`), write one notification to the inviter — "Maria, who you brought here, just had her help confirmed on the Oak Street garden." — with href to that project. Once per invitee, ever, enforced by the first-confirmation condition rather than a counter. Surface the same fact on the profile deeds record beside the existing 🌟 Brought the Neighbors badge, which already counts `invited_by` rows in `src/lib/badges.ts`.

**Why now.** The operator's own growth loop runs on personal invite links and clan codes. This is the only moment that tells an inviter their invite became a real act of help, and it fires identically in a neighborhood of five and one of five thousand.

**What could go wrong.** It makes one person's deed partly somebody else's news. Keep it strictly private to the inviter, never rendered on the invitee's page, and never expressed as a count — "you've produced 4 helpers" is an activity-volume metric and fails INCENTIVES §1.2 outright. Firing on later contributions would turn a warm fact into a drip; the first-confirmation guard is the whole safety design.

## Invitation and growth

How a neighborhood actually spreads: on paper, through groups, one person at a time.

### The invitation doorstep — a public /i/[code] landing

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** A clan link (/clans?clan=<code>) and a personal link (/login?via=<uuid>) both land the invited person on the same generic login page. src/app/login/page.tsx shows every visitor the same nine global-reach ideas and the same pulse counts — no inviter's name, no community, no reason it is about them. The one moment someone is paying attention because a person they trust sent them something, and the page says nothing about that person.

**How.** New public route src/app/i/[code]/page.tsx, with path.startsWith('/i/') added to isPublicRoute in src/lib/supabase/proxy.ts; the proxy already writes ?clan= and ?via= into the pa-clan / pa-via cookies, so do the same from the path segment and the existing attribution path in src/app/page.tsx (profiles.invited_by, join_clan) keeps working untouched. New migration 0050 adds invite_context(p_code text) returns jsonb — security definer, modelled line for line on welcome_context in 0048 — resolving the code against clans.invite_code (and neighborhoods.invite_token, see the group-import idea) and returning: inviter display_name, clan name, community name + city, verified-neighbor count, the next three city_events rows, the count of open offers where kind='need' and claimed_by is null, and titles of projects where reach='global'. That is exactly the disclosure ceiling migrations 0042 and 0043 already set for /city/[slug]. Grant to anon, plus an ip-hash rate cap in the pattern of frontier_request_log. The page reads 'Alexandre invited you to Aurora. Saturday: park cleanup at 10. Two people need a hand this week.' — sign-up card underneath the proof, not above it.

**Why now.** With five neighbors the invite is the entire funnel, and the inviter is the only credibility the product has. A stranger's landing page can afford to be generic; a friend's cannot. It also feeds the poster and the invitation email, so one RPC fixes three surfaces.

**What could go wrong.** A forwarded link publishes a community's headcount and a real person's display name to anyone who receives it — the ceiling must equal /city/[slug]'s (counts plus global-reach titles, never a neighbor list, never a neighborhood-scoped project title). Worse failure: an honest doorstep for a two-person community reads 'two people are here' and converts worse than today's generic page. Suppress the headcount below a floor and lead with the one dated thing instead.

### Invite someone to a thing, not to the app

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** Everything shareable today is an invitation to a product. Nobody invites a neighbor to a product — they invite them to Saturday at ten, or ask them for twenty minutes. Events live only inside a signed-in project page and small-help asks only on /people#asks, so there is literally nothing to send someone who has no account.

**How.** Migration 0050: `alter table events add column invite_token text unique` and the same on offers (kind='need'), null by default — the token is the consent to be seen by a stranger, so nothing becomes public until the host presses a button. Mint/revoke server actions in src/app/projects/actions.ts (founder or co-organizer, same guard as event creation) and src/app/offers/offerActions.ts (own rows only). Public routes src/app/e/[token]/page.tsx and src/app/h/[token]/page.tsx read anon-safe views (public_invited_event, public_invited_ask) exposing title, starts_at or when_text, the free-text place, host first name and the rsvps count — never lat/lng, since the ~110 m blunting in UX_SPEC is a privacy floor, not a substitute for consent. The ask flavour carries a derived line, not a written one: if nothing else has been minted for that ask, 'Alexandre asked you for 20 minutes on Saturday. You're the first person he asked.' Sign-up lands straight on the RSVP or the claim through the existing pa-via handoff in src/app/page.tsx.

**Why now.** A five-person neighborhood has no audience, but every person in it has a phone full of neighbors. This turns each real event and each real need into its own front door, and it is the only invitation that costs the sender nothing socially — they are sharing a plan, not recommending an app.

**What could go wrong.** A token forwarded onward publishes a time and a place to strangers. Mitigations are load-bearing: show the host the exact stranger-view before they copy, put 'revoke this link' beside it, never render the map pin. The 'first person he asked' line becomes manipulation the moment it is not true, so derive it from the token count or drop it entirely. Rate-cap minting through user_action_log (migration 0038) so it cannot become bulk outreach.

### The noticeboard kit — a printable sheet with a QR and tear-off tabs

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** Hyperlocal spreads on paper: the library corkboard, the school gate, the laundromat, the coffee shop window. There is no printable artifact anywhere in the codebase — no QR library, no print CSS, no OG image. The one printable blessed in INCENTIVES §3.6 is a post-pilot 'Founded by' celebration at 10 neighbors, which is the wrong artifact for a place with three. An operator running many cities alone has nothing to hand a librarian.

**How.** src/app/poster/page.tsx (signed in; community from community_members, and admins reach any community through the existing city switcher behind src/app/admin/city). One Letter/A4 sheet in a @media print stylesheet — black on white, no background fills, no rail — plus a window.print() button; no PDF library and no download, the browser's dialog does it. Content comes from the same invite_context RPC: community name, the next dated thing from events or city_events, one open ask with its honest time estimate ('20 min · Saturday'), one line of what's being built, and a QR of SITE_URL/i/<code> rendered as inline SVG by a small src/lib/qr.ts or the qrcode package at build time (the CSP allows no external image host anyway). A strip of eight mini-QR tear-off tabs at the foot, because that is the format that actually works on a corkboard. A second layout prints /city/[slug], which is already public and in the sitemap, so that QR needs no account at all.

**Why now.** This is the only growth channel that works at five neighbors, and it is the one the owner has to execute in person in every city. Generating the sheet from live data is the difference between running one city by hand and running ten.

**What could go wrong.** An honest poster in a two-person community reads '2 neighbors' and kills the thing it advertises. The sheet must lead with the dated event or the open ask, suppress the headcount below a floor, and — if there is no dated thing at all — refuse to print and say why ('put something on the calendar first; a poster for an empty week brings nobody'). Second risk: print CSS is where a good screen design quietly falls apart, so this needs testing on real paper, not a print preview.

### Bring a group you're already in, in one step

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** A book club, a street WhatsApp group, a church team already has the people; it just has no page. Today the organizer opens NewCommunityDialog, fills four fields, lands on an empty community with one member, and then has to invite everyone individually — because communities have no join link at all. Only clans got invite codes in migration 0046. Every step is a place to give up, and the end state is an emptier room than the group chat they came from.

**How.** Migration 0050: `alter table neighborhoods add column invite_token text unique default replace(gen_random_uuid()::text,'-','')` plus a join_community_by_token(p_token) RPC shaped exactly like join_clan in 0046, so a community becomes linkable the way a clan already is. Add a 'Bring a group you're already in' door to the five-door composer chooser, opening a three-step variant of src/app/people/NewCommunityDialog.tsx that: (1) calls the existing createCommunity in src/app/neighborhood/communityActions.ts; (2) refuses to finish without one first thing — a rhythm written to projects.when_text (migration 0037's six flip cards, e.g. 'Thursdays') or a dated events row; (3) outputs one paste-ready message rather than a list of email fields: 'Our book club has a page now — Thursdays, and you can see who's coming: peoplearound.com/i/<token>'. Copy button, nothing else. The /i/<token> landing then shows the group's own name and its rhythm, so the person arriving from the group chat recognises their own group rather than a product.

**Why now.** This is the fastest path from zero to a real five-person community, and it imports trust instead of manufacturing it. It is also the only mechanic here that does not depend on strangers at all.

**What could go wrong.** A half-migrated group is worse than no group — 3 of 12 join, the page looks deader than the chat, and the organizer looks foolish for having asked. Requiring a rhythm or a date before the link exists is the main mitigation; hiding the member count below a floor is the second. And WhatsApp group admins are a scarce, easily-burned resource: if the first one in a town has a bad experience, that is the town.

### The bring-one moment, at the point of commitment

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** Nothing asks a joiner to bring anyone. The founding banner (INCENTIVES §2.3) asks the founder, and retires at 10 neighbors — so the person who has just said 'I'm coming Saturday', at the exact moment their motivation peaks and they finally have something concrete to talk about, is asked nothing at all.

**How.** Extend the two celebratory beats that already exist: the '🎉 You're on the team' moment in the join flow (src/app/projects/[id]/page.tsx) and the RSVP confirmation on the event card. One quiet line beneath it — 'Anyone you'd bring with you?' — opening the event's stranger-link from the invite-token idea with a pre-written, fully editable message naming the event, the date and the place, and never naming the product in the first sentence. Shown once per event per user and dismissed forever after (localStorage, the same pattern already used by BadgeCelebration), with no counter, no reward, no follow-up.

**Why now.** At five people the only person who can credibly recruit the sixth is the fifth, and only about something specific. This is the founding banner's 'collective, finite mission' logic moved from the founder to the joiner, where it is cheaper and truer.

**What could go wrong.** This is the idea closest to the line. A prompt after every commitment is a growth nag, which UX_SPEC §6 forbids by name. The fences are non-optional: it attaches only to a thing with a date (never a star, never a profile), it is one line and never a modal, and it never returns once ignored. If it cannot be built so that a person who ignores it is never asked again, it should not ship.

### Invitation receipts — news about the person you brought, not a count

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** The only feedback an inviter gets today is a number: 'You've brought 3 neighbors here already' on the founding-era banner. A count is the shape of a growth metric, and it quietly makes inviting feel like recruiting. INCENTIVES §3.4 already approved the alternative and it has never been built.

**How.** Migration 0050 adds a trigger on contributions (status → confirmed) that writes one row into notifications — the trigger fan-out from migration 0025 already exists — addressed to profiles.invited_by of the contributor, deduped by a unique partial index so it can never become a stream. Copy: 'Maria — who you brought here — just helped complete the garden.' No number, no rank, no page listing 'your invitees'. Consent belongs to the invitee, not the inviter: a profiles.invite_receipts_opt_out control in src/app/settings, and the trigger fires only on acts already visible to the whole community — never DMs, asks, offers, or anything private.

**Why now.** The product's whole thesis is that recognition should be a true fact about a person rather than a score. Applying that to invitation is the difference between 'I brought 3 users' and 'someone I brought is helping on Saturday', and only the second makes a person want to invite a fourth. It also costs almost nothing: the data and the fan-out already exist.

**What could go wrong.** Get the consent wrong and it is surveillance. The inviter learns something about the invitee's behaviour, and the invitee never asked to be in a reporting relationship. Opt-out is the weak version; the honest version asks at sign-up, which may be too heavy for onboarding. If it is, restrict the trigger to confirmed contributions only — a confirmed contribution is already a public, co-attested fact about that person, so nothing new is disclosed.

### The operator's invitation, with an actual neighborhood in it

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** inviteRealResident in src/app/admin/cityActions.ts calls admin.auth.admin.inviteUserByEmail, so the person receives Supabase's default 'You have been invited' template — no neighborhood name, no events, no sender, no reason. Meanwhile the welcome email sent thirty minutes after sign-up (src/lib/welcome.ts + welcome_context, migration 0048) is warm and specific. The good email arrives after the decision; the empty one carries it. And it sends one address at a time, which does not survive a library handing over a thirty-name list.

**How.** Stop using the Supabase template for invitations. Queue them the way welcomes are queued: an invite_mail_jobs table modelled on welcome_mail_jobs (migrations 0046/0048), drained by the same lease-and-claim worker pattern, keeping the Resend Idempotency-Key discipline already in src/lib/welcome.ts so a retry can never become a second email. Build the body from the same invite_context(community) RPC the landing page and the poster use — community name, the next three real dated things from city_events and events, one open ask, and a /i/<token> link — so the email, the page and the printed sheet all say the same true sentences. Add bulk paste to src/app/admin/GrowthTools.tsx: a textarea of addresses plus one community, capped and de-duplicated against profiles, with a preview of the exact email and recipient list before anything sends.

**Why now.** One operator, many cities. This is the channel the owner is already working by hand, and it currently sends the least persuasive message the product is capable of producing. Fixing it is mostly wiring between two things that already exist.

**What could go wrong.** Bulk invitation is one bad list away from being spam, and a place-based product cannot recover a reputation for it. Hard requirements: a per-send cap, a visible confirmation of exactly what goes to exactly whom, a one-click unsubscribe that also blocks re-invitation, and a refusal to import a list the operator did not get consent for. Separately, inviteUserByEmail creates an auth user before the person has agreed to anything — a stranger's address becoming a row is itself worth being careful about, and /privacy should say so plainly.

## Institutions as the shortcut

Libraries and parks departments already have the room, the noticeboard and the trust.

### The org record and the ten-minute claim link

*Effort: L*

**The problem.** Every institutional yes currently costs the operator a personal relationship plus a manual admin action, and it dies when that one staff member moves desks. A librarian cannot install an app, cannot sign a data agreement, and often cannot create an account on a third-party service without IT approval — so "sign up and post your events" is a no before it is finished being read.

**How.** New migration 0050_partner_orgs.sql: partner_orgs(id, city_id → event_cities, community_id → neighborhoods nullable, name, slug via public.city_slug (0043), kind check in ('library','parks','school','museum','civic','nonprofit','business'), website, address, contact_email, claim_token_hash, claim_expires_at, claimed_at, claimed_name, verified_by → profiles). Service-role writes only, mirroring event_cities in 0045; reads for authenticated gated by a can_read_city_events-style predicate. Admin mints the row and the token in a new src/app/admin/PartnerOrgs.tsx + partnerActions.ts, both behind requireAdministrator() from src/lib/admin.ts — same shape as the existing sourceActions.ts. The operator emails the link; /partner/[token]/page.tsx is a session-less console, added to isPublicRoute in src/lib/supabase/proxy.ts alongside /city/. The console shows what we publish about them, an on/off switch for their calendar feed (idea 2), a preview of their public page (idea 3), the embed snippet (idea 4), and a contact field. It never posts as the org, never names a resident, never reads resident data.

**Why now.** One operator running many cities cannot hold a relationship per branch. A token link is the only kind of yes that survives a staff change and costs one email to renew. In a place with five neighbors, a library with a room, a noticeboard and a mailing list is worth more than fifty sign-ups.

**What could go wrong.** A token in an email is a bearer credential — forwarded to a staff listserv, anyone on it can flip the feed. Fence it: the token can only toggle a feed an admin already approved, preview, and request a contact change (which emails the old address); deleting the org or editing the embed domain allowlist needs a fresh admin-minted link. Second, softer failure: the console makes the operator feel a partnership exists when nobody ever opened the link. Record claimed_at and treat unclaimed orgs as leads, never as partners, in every count.

### Publish into the app, instead of being crawled

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** We find a library's calendar by search and fetch it (migrations 0045/0049). However politely it is done, it is something happening to them: they cannot correct a wrong listing, cannot stop it, and were never asked. It is also the weakest possible opening line, and exactly the thing a comms officer says no to.

**How.** Add org_id → partner_orgs to city_event_sources, and 'partner' to the city_events provider check (0049 already widened it once, to 'calendar'). At /partner/[token]/calendar the org pastes the .ics or calendar page they already publish and presses "show me what you'd read" — crawlNextCalendar() in src/lib/city-events/crawler.ts runs once against that single row and renders exactly what it produced (title, date label, venue, source link) before anything goes live, with the same sourceUrl() validation from normalize.ts and the robots/DNS-pinning/1MB guards in safe-fetch.ts. Listings carry source_name = org.name and render in src/app/events/CityEvents.tsx as "published by Aurora Central Library" rather than found-on-the-web. The off switch sets enabled=false and expires their rows. Then the conversion path: an action on src/app/admin/CalendarSources.tsx turns an already-discovered source row into an org plus a claim invitation, so the outreach email opens "we are already listing 14 of your events — here is the page; do you want to own it and fix what's wrong?"

**Why now.** That opening line converts far better than a cold pitch, and the machinery is already shipped — this is consent and labelling on top of it. It also closes the open item in FEATURE_IDEAS §21 (visitkc.com, kclibrary.org publish no structured data): an org that owns its row can paste an .ics we would never have discovered.

**What could go wrong.** Institutional programming is high-volume and reliable; neighbor-led ideas are neither. If a library's forty weekly events land anywhere near the project feed, the product quietly becomes a listings site and the mission is gone. Keep them inside "Around your city" as CITY_EVENT_IMPORTS already rules, cap what one org shows per week, and never let a listing take an RSVP, a join, or contribution credit. Second: a partner feed arrives full of marketing copy — strip to title/date/venue/link and keep the crawler's existing refusal to copy HTML or images.

### /place/[slug] — the page a partner is proud to link to

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** /city/[slug] names nobody by design, which is right for privacy and useless as a reason for a branch manager to link to us. An institution needs a page with their own name on it that shows something true happened because of them — something they can paste into a quarterly report.

**How.** New anon-safe views in the 0043 style (security_invoker = off, counts only): public_places (slug, name, kind, city slug, live partner listings, projects that named this venue, projects still building) and public_place_ideas, reusing the reach = 'global' filter public_city_ideas uses so a neighborhood-scoped project can never leak. Route src/app/place/[slug]/page.tsx copies the structure of src/app/city/[slug]/page.tsx — Stat tiles, category bars, "Open to anyone" — plus a "on their calendar this week" strip and a link back to /city/[slug]. Add "/place/" to the allow list in src/app/robots.ts, the slugs to src/app/sitemap.ts (through the anon key, exactly as citySlugs() does), and path.startsWith("/place/") to isPublicRoute in src/lib/supabase/proxy.ts. The city page gains a "Places that host this" row.

**Why now.** It is the cheapest possible reciprocity. We are asking a public body for its noticeboard and mailing list; a URL with their name on it is what we have to trade, and it costs nothing per city once the view exists.

**What could go wrong.** An org page with nothing on it is worse than no page — it makes us look dead at the exact moment we are being evaluated. Gate publication: 404 (or token-private) until there is something real to show. Harder rule: never publish a page in an institution's name before a human there has claimed it — a page auto-generated from a crawled calendar is us speaking for them, the same sin as an undisclosed fake user. And hold the no-advertiser line: one website link, one address, no products, no fundraising, no sponsorship, and a business-kind org says on the page that it is a business.

### The embed, and the CSP line that currently forbids it

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** Tier 3 item 18 has never been built, and today it cannot be: src/lib/csp.ts ships frame-ancestors 'none' with a comment saying to revisit it if the widget is ever built. Meanwhile a library or school website is exactly where the people who are on no app already look.

**How.** src/app/embed/[slug]/page.tsx — server-rendered, no client JS, no cookies, no session, styles inline: the next five things near that place (same anon-safe views as idea 3, plus public_city_ideas), three neighbor-led ideas open to anyone, and one line — "Something you want to start here? →". partner_orgs.embed_domains text[] holds the origins the org registered; the route sets frame-ancestors to those exact origins by branching on pathname in buildCsp() (or returning a per-route header from updateSession in src/lib/supabase/proxy.ts) — never by relaxing the global policy, never '*'. Cache-Control: public, s-maxage=600 so a busy library page costs nothing. Outbound links carry ?place=<slug>; the proxy stamps a pa-place cookie right beside the existing pa-via handling, and sign-up writes profiles.arrived_via_org once, non-overwritable, exactly as invited_by works in migration 0016.

**Why now.** An embed is a permanent frontier funnel into a place we cannot otherwise reach, and it costs the librarian one paste. It is also the only item here that recruits from entirely outside the app, which is what a five-person neighborhood actually needs.

**What could go wrong.** A widget rendering an empty list on a partner's homepage is an advertisement for a dead product, and we would never see it. Give it a scope ladder — place → city → a real "be the first here" invitation — and put a private "rendered N times, showed nothing M of them" line in the operator's admin, never on the partner's page. Second: framing is a security surface. Keep it cookie-less, no postMessage, no resize script, no third-party requests, and never render anything a signed-out visitor could not already read at /city/[slug].

### The room is the thing an institution can actually give

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** We keep asking institutions to promote us. The valuable trade runs the other way: the most common reason a neighborhood project dies is having nowhere to meet, and a branch or a parks department already lends rooms, tables, storage and noticeboards for free. Nothing lets an institution offer one, and nothing lets a project say where it meets.

**How.** alter type public.offer_kind add value 'space' (the one-line pattern 0033 used for 'need'), plus offers.org_id → partner_orgs. Deliberately keep offers.user_id: a named staff member posts it and the org is the attribution, so there is a human to email when it goes stale and we never invent a poster who is not a person. Two shipped triggers need exemptions — blunt_offer_location (0030) must not round an org's coordinates, because a library's address is public and a room blunted to 110 m is useless; and set_offer_neighborhood (0027) stamps the poster's own neighborhood, which is wrong when the librarian lives two towns over, so stamp org.community_id when org_id is set. On the project side, projects.venue_org_id lets a founder say "we meet at the Central branch" — which is what fills the partner page in idea 3 and tells the librarian the one thing they want to know: the room is being used.

**Why now.** It makes the partnership two-way in the first conversation. "Will you list the room you already lend?" is a far easier yes than "will you tell your patrons about our app", and Aurora's registered-group program (OUTREACH_TARGETS Tier 3) already gives groups of 3+ free library and fire-station rooms — we would be surfacing a benefit the city is already funding.

**What could go wrong.** Rot. The staff member who posted it leaves, and a neighbor turns up at a room reassigned two years ago — the worst possible first experience of the product. Quarterly "is this still true?" email to contact_email; an org offer unconfirmed for 180 days greys out rather than vanishing, and is never framed as a failure. Second: a commercial venue using 'space' as a wedge for paid programming — org kind is visible, promotional copy and paid links are out, there is no booking and no money anywhere, and a claim just reveals their existing booking instructions.

### The yes kit: /for/libraries, /for/parks, and a printable noticeboard sheet

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** A public employee's yes is blocked by questions nobody has written down: what data do you take about our patrons, what does it cost, does it need procurement, is it accessible, what will you put in our name, and can we stop. Those answers currently live in the founder's head and in three email templates in docs/MARKETING.md. Meanwhile the library's strongest asset — the physical corkboard — is the one channel this product cannot reach at all.

**How.** Two public routes modelled on src/app/start/page.tsx (already in isPublicRoute): /for/libraries and /for/parks, allowlisted in src/app/robots.ts and listed in src/app/sitemap.ts. The content is the answer sheet in writing, linking /privacy: free, no contract, no procurement, we never receive patron data, we publish counts and never a resident's name (0043's rule is quotable as-is), you can switch your feed off from your own link, and here is exactly what your page looks like. Second half: src/app/place/[slug]/poster/page.tsx — a print-CSS Letter/A4 page with the neighborhood's name, three real current things (falling back to three starters from src/lib/playbooks.ts when the place is new), a QR to /city/[slug]?place=<slug>, and a date stamp. No PDF library; the browser's print dialog is the export.

**Why now.** It is the cheapest item on this list and it unblocks every other one — a partner page and an embed are worthless if the librarian cannot get the yes past their manager. And the poster is the only thing here that reaches someone who is not already online, which in a five-person neighborhood is nearly everybody.

**What could go wrong.** A printed sheet is stale the day after it prints, and a QR pointing at an empty place is a wasted first impression — hence the date stamp, the playbook fallback, and pointing the QR at /city/[slug], which is honest about being small, rather than at a feed a signed-out visitor cannot see. Second, and this is the real one: writing the promises down makes them binding. That is the point, but it means "we never see patron data" has to stay true forever — no analytics on the embed beyond the single non-overwritable arrived_via_org stamp.

### Honest partnership measurement, and the list of things we will never show

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** The operator needs to know whether a partnership did anything, and the partner needs a line for their quarterly report. Both pressures point straight at a per-resident funnel — arrivals, conversion, "your patrons" — which would break the promise that makes a public-sector yes possible and turn residents into the metric.

**How.** One private page at /partner/[token]/report, plus a section on the existing /admin/activity (migration 0047). Everything derived: listings the org published and how many are live; neighbor-led projects that named them as venue; how many of those reached state = 'building' or completed; how many things actually got built. One sentence written to be quoted — "Three neighbor-led projects met at this branch this quarter; two are still running." What it must never contain, written into the migration comment the way 0043's rules are: no resident names, no counts of individuals, no view or click data (views are private and owner-only, UX §4.15), no export, no cohort. arrived_via_org from idea 4 stays operator-side only — it is about a person's arrival, and no partner needs it. The ROADMAP anti-metric applies: if arrivals via a partner rise and attested contributions do not, the partnership is decoration, and that judgement belongs to the operator, not to the partner's page.

**Why now.** The first partner will ask "how is it going?" within a month, and whatever gets built in a hurry to answer that is what every later partner inherits. Deciding the shape now — one operator, no pressure, no partner yet — is far cheaper than walking a promise back.

**What could go wrong.** Any number becomes a target. A parks officer whose page shows one project may read it as failure and quietly drop us, so lead with the sentence and what got built, keep counts small and late (UX §5, restraint with metrics), and never show one partner a comparison against another — that is the no-individual-leaderboard rule applied to a surface where it has not been tested yet. Second: an org report is a new place where a resident could be re-identified by inference — three projects at one branch in one quarter is nearly nameable. Publish only counts already visible on the public page, and keep project titles out unless they are reach = 'global'.

## The first ninety seconds

What a person meets when they are the only one in their city.

### City listings on the front door

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** The imports already work — city_events fills weekly per city — but the only surface that renders them is /events via src/app/events/CityEvents.tsx. The page a new account actually lands on is /people (src/app/page.tsx redirects there), whose "Happening soon" strip comes from loadFeedCards in src/lib/feed.ts, which reads only the neighbor-led `events` table. So the first ninety seconds in Kansas City show an empty room while dozens of true, dated, local listings sit one RLS-scoped query away, three taps behind a rail item a newcomer never presses.

**How.** Extract CityEvents' query into src/lib/cityFeed.ts as upcomingCityListings(supabase, limit), reusing distinctListings from src/lib/city-events/normalize.ts (RLS via can_read_city_events already scopes to your city). Render it as a third strip in src/app/people/page.tsx and src/app/explore/page.tsx, below the existing "Neighbors need a hand" and "Happening soon" blocks, keeping the separate heading and the "listings we found, not neighbors' plans — check the organizer's page" line so the honesty boundary DATA_MODEL draws around event_cities survives the move. When cards.length === 0, this strip renders above the empty-state card and the empty-state copy at src/app/people/page.tsx:482-489 changes from "Nothing in your communities yet" to something true: "No neighbor has started anything in Kansas City yet — here's what the city itself has on this week."

**Why now.** It is the only real, local, time-stamped content a one-person city possesses, and it is already being fetched, normalized and deduplicated by code that ships today. Nothing new has to be true about the world for this to work at N=1.

**What could go wrong.** We start reading as an events aggregator, and four scraped rows out-volume the one thing a neighbor actually made. Cap at 4 rows, always render below anything neighbor-led, never let listings into the map pins or the weekly digest. Second risk: a city whose importer has not run yet gets an empty strip too — render nothing rather than "no listings yet", which is a second apology on a page that already has one.

### Turn a listing into a plan

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** A listing is something to attend alone. Nothing converts "there is a thing happening" into "we are going together", which is the actual product. Meanwhile the only day-one action we offer a person with zero neighbors is the five-door composer, which asks them to invent something and then recruit for it.

**How.** Migration 0050 adds `events.city_event_id uuid references public.city_events(id)` plus `create unique index on public.events(city_event_id) where city_event_id is not null` — one plan per listing, so five arrivals converge instead of fragmenting into five plans for the same concert. A server action (src/app/events/planFromListing.ts, invoked from the strip in idea 1) creates a `projects` row (category social, state active, reach 'city', description prefilled with the listing title, venue and a link to source_url), an `events` row with starts_at/place copied from the listing, and the creator's own `rsvps` row. When the unique index already holds a plan, the button instead reads "2 neighbors are going — join them" and only inserts the RSVP.

**Why now.** It is the cheapest honest first action available in an empty city: you were going to the free library talk anyway, saying so costs nothing, and it leaves behind a joinable object with a date, a project page and a history timeline that the second arrival finds three weeks later. It also gives "Happening soon" on /people a genuine neighbor-led row without anyone inventing anything.

**What could go wrong.** The creator flakes and a stranger stands outside a venue alone — the one place this product can produce a genuinely bad in-person experience. Mitigate in copy: the RSVP says "you're going too", never "meet Alex"; speaking first confers no organizer status; a plan still holding a single RSVP the day before quietly stops being promoted. Second risk: scraped data is wrong. Keep the "check the organizer's page ↗" link inline on the plan itself, and if the source row flips to status 'cancelled' the project page must say so rather than silently rotting.

### The arrival promise

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** The first person in Kansas City posts something and the product goes silent for weeks — nothing they can do changes that. Worse, src/lib/welcome.ts mails them, thirty minutes after signup, the literal sentence "0 other verified neighbor accounts are here". Accurate, clinical, and the only thing we ever say to someone about being early.

**How.** Three pieces. (a) At post time, when the poster's community has fewer than ~3 members, the confirmation says the true thing: "Nobody is here yet to read this. It stays. We'll show it to the next person who joins Kansas City, and email you the day they do." (b) Migration 0050 adds 'neighbor_arrived' to the notifications.kind check constraint (migration 0025 line 24 — a trigger inserting an unlisted kind fails today) plus a trigger on community_members that, for members 2 through 10, calls notify() for every earlier member: "Someone just joined Kansas City — you're no longer the only one." The existing */10 /api/push cron delivers it to anyone opted in; the Saturday /api/digest carries it otherwise. (c) Extend welcome_context(p_user) (migration 0048) to return the community's first project or event, and rewrite the empty-city branch in src/lib/welcome.ts to lead with it: "Kansas City has one neighbor so far — {name}, who put up {thing}."

**Why now.** It converts the worst property of a cold start — nothing ever changes — into the one event that is guaranteed to eventually happen, and it makes the first person's post worth making by promising it an audience. Part (c) also makes the second person's first action easier: the first thing they are shown is a real neighbor's work, not a labelled demo profile.

**What could go wrong.** If nobody ever arrives, a promise makes the silence worse than it would have been unpromised. So it must be bounded and never repeated: one line at post time, no reminder mail, no "still waiting" nudge — that would be exactly the punitive-absence mechanic INCENTIVES §1.4 forbids. Second risk: notifying member #1 about member #2 is one step from surveillance. The body names the place filling up, not the person's activity, and it fires once per arrival, never again for anything they do.

### One door in an empty room

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** src/components/FeedComposer.tsx opens a five-door chooser. Five choices is right for a live neighborhood and paralysing at zero — each door asks "what will you bring?" of someone with no evidence anyone is listening. And at the bottom of the same page, src/components/DemoResidents.tsx prints eight labelled names with no content attached, which tells a newcomer nothing about what a post here even looks like.

**How.** (a) FeedComposer takes an `empty` prop computed in src/app/people/page.tsx from values already loaded there (hoodSize, cards.length). In empty mode the strip shows one primary door — the standing-invitation flow at /projects/new?intent=meet, because a thing you already do weekly is the only thing a person can honestly offer before anyone is around — with "something else" revealing the other four. (b) Replace the demo-resident name grid with three or four labelled example posts rendered inline where real cards go, in unmistakably different chrome (dashed border, muted, an "Example — we wrote this, nobody posted it" chip), each with "post one like this" opening the right composer prefilled. Source them from a static src/lib/examples.ts, not from demo_residents: an example needs no table, no RLS story and no seeding cron.

**Why now.** This is the literal first ninety seconds, it is the cheapest of the seven, and it converts a surface that today only labels its own emptiness into one that teaches the shape of a post that gets answered — the same job the playbooks' "what to ask for" lists do for projects.

**What could go wrong.** Hiding four doors is a bet on which first action works; if it is the wrong bet we have narrowed everyone onto it. Keep the others one tap away and keep empty mode strictly conditional on a genuinely empty community. Second risk: examples rendered in feed position can be misread as content at a glance — they must never be countable, never link through to a fake project page, and never sit within visual distance of a real card's styling.

### Meeting spots

*Effort: M*

**The problem.** There is no place object anywhere in the schema. events.place and offers.place are free text; offer/ask lat-lng is deliberately blunted to ~110 m; /faves ranks starred projects, not places. So in a new city the map shows exactly one pin — peopleClusterPins in src/lib/mapPins.ts returns your community centroid labelled "1 neighbor" — which is a map of nowhere, and every composer asks "where?" of someone typing it from memory with no shared vocabulary to draw on.

**How.** Migration 0050 adds `places` (id, community_id → neighborhoods, name, kind in park/library/cafe/court/hall/other, lat, lng, note ≤200 chars, added_by, created_at) with the RLS shape offers already uses: readable in your communities, insertable by members, editable by the author. A two-minute solo flow at a new /places route (or folded into /faves): "Where do people already gather here? Name three." Pins land on the shared MapShell through a new placePins() in src/lib/mapPins.ts. v1 is table + route + pins; the follow-up wires a "pick a spot" selector into the event, ask and offer composers so they fill `place` and the pin together. OSM/Overpass seeding is possible later, but v1 is human-entered on purpose.

**Why now.** It is real local knowledge no scraper has, it takes one person two minutes with nobody else present, and it makes every later action cheaper — an ask, an event and a plan-from-listing all get an answer to "where?". It also gives the map something honest on day one instead of a single dot over the city centre.

**What could go wrong.** This drifts into Yelp the moment it acquires ratings, reviews, photos or business listings — so no rating field ever, no ordering by popularity, note is one line about meeting there, and the framing stays "somewhere neighbors can meet", never "places in town". Second risk: someone names a spot that is private or unsafe to gather at; keep the offers-era honesty ("a corner or a block") and let any member correct a spot. Third: it is another table on a schema that already carries 49 migrations, and the composer integrations are where the scope quietly doubles.

### Elsewhere, by distance

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** A person in an empty city has no evidence the pattern works anywhere. Every proof we own lives in other cities, and both obvious ways to show it are wrong: hiding it leaves the product looking dead, and mixing it into the feed implies it is local.

**How.** A footer band under the empty feed on /people, reading the anon-safe views migration 0043 already ships — public_cities (city, slug, communities, projects, neighbors) and public_city_ideas (global-reach titles only) — joined to event_cities.lat/lng for coordinates, ordered by great-circle distance from the viewer's own neighborhoods.center_lat/lng, nearest first. Every row carries its distance so it cannot be misread: "Aurora, CO · 1,010 km from you · 6 projects, 34 neighbors", linking to the public /city/[slug] page that already exists. Lead with a sentence that names what it is: "Nothing here is near you. This is what other cities have built — the same way yours will." The band disappears the moment their own community has anything in it.

**Why now.** It answers "is this thing real?" with data that is already public and already anon-safe, and the destination page is built. It is the only way to show momentum without the map or the feed pretending it is local.

**What could go wrong.** Sorting places by activity is a hair from a leaderboard of communities — forbidden for individuals outright and against the spirit of INCENTIVES §1.3 for places. So sort by distance only, never by counts, and never render a rank number. Second risk: "Aurora has 34 neighbors" reads as "I am behind", which is precisely the feeling this product exists to remove; the copy must frame evidence, not comparison. Third: with few cities live the nearest may be 1,500 km away, which reads as absence, not momentum — in that case show one completed project's story instead of a list of distant counts.

### Send your city page to one institution

*Effort: S*

**The problem.** /city/[slug] exists, is readable signed out, is listed in src/app/sitemap.ts, and was built precisely for a librarian or an HOA board — yet nothing inside the product ever shows a signed-in resident their own city's page or suggests sending it. docs/OUTREACH_TARGETS.md is the operator's spreadsheet; the person actually standing in Kansas City has the local relationships the operator does not.

**How.** Link /city/{slug of profile.neighborhood.city} from the founding-era banner in src/app/people/page.tsx (lines 316-354, beside the existing CopyLinkButton) and from /invite, framed as "the page you can send to someone who runs something here". Beside it, one prefilled copy/mailto block in the shape MARKETING.md's template A already uses — free infrastructure for the layer they are weakest at, never "use our app" — carrying the city page URL. Count nothing about it: this is not a referral program and must not become one.

**Why now.** The highest-leverage act available to one person alone on day one is not posting, it is bringing an institution that already holds the neighbors. The page for that audience is built and currently unreachable from inside the product.

**What could go wrong.** On a first day it can read as homework handed to a lonely person — so it belongs beside the founding banner, not in the empty feed, and never as a checklist item that sits unticked forever. Second risk: an empty /city/[slug] is a bad first impression for the institution and burns the contact; gate the link on the page actually having something (≥1 project, or ≥1 community with a description) and point at /playbooks until then. Distinct from the pipeline's post-pilot printed "Founded by" poster (INCENTIVES §3.6) — that is physical and at 10 neighbors; this is digital and at one.
